0. The recommendation on one screen

Allkept already sends three kinds of push notification (a save was sorted, a save needs you, news about the subscription) and schedules reminders that people set on a save. Before adding anything, five problems in what exists should be fixed, because they make today's notifications impossible to switch off from the app, start them without a clear yes, or send one notification per save during an import. After that, build one small gate that every notification passes through, then add new kinds one at a time, starting with the ones that answer something the person started.

What to send

  1. Answers to something the person started, on by default once notifications are on: "Your trip plan is ready" (today the plan takes two to five minutes and people are told they can leave); "Your older saves are sorted", one note per import instead of one per save; "A save needs its link", only when they are the one who can fix it and Instagram's reply hasn't already told them.
  2. Things the person asked for: the reminders they set on a save (these exist), and, for a trip with dates, two reminders they agree to when they enter the dates.
  3. From your library, off until the person says yes: one weekly note, "Your week in Allkept", which also carries one older save worth another look and a count of new interests.
  4. Not now: "come back" notes, "Planning a trip?" pushes (that stays an in-app card, per queue item 59), standalone interest pushes, badges, iOS "deliver quietly", Time Sensitive.

How often

At most 5 a week per person, not counting reminders they set or account notices; of those, at most 2 that Allkept starts on its own; at most 2 a day; nothing Allkept starts between 21:00 and 08:00 their time, and nothing it starts in their first week. Lock-screen text never names a save, a place, a person or an interest unless the person turns on Show save details.

In what order to build

Phase 0 fixes → one gate (a send log, preferences, caps) → Trips → Sorting v2 → trip-date reminders → the weekly note → recording which save was opened → away notes, only if Pranav decides to. None of it needs a new native build: everything uses modules already in build 34. It ships through migrations Pranav runs, function deploys and over-the-air updates, each on his explicit yes.

1.4 What is wrong or missing

F1 · The notification switches cannot be saved from the app. High Verified in the live database; consequence Inferred
On 2 October the database reports that the signed-in role may not update notify_enabled, notify_sorted, notify_attention or ai_sorting_enabled (it may update interests_enabled, language and display_name). The cause is in the migrations: 20260909124351_profile_onboarding.sql L52–54 removed the general right to update profiles and granted it back for five named columns; the switches were added the next day without a grant. The app writes them directly (lib/preferences.ts), and a refused write rolls back silently (L80–83). So turning notifications off deletes the device, the preference stays on, and Settings registers the device again straight away (settings.tsx L69–72): notifications cannot really be switched off from inside Allkept, only from the phone's own settings. ERRORS.md (13 September) records a hand test in which a switch held, so either a grant existed then and has since gone, or that test misled. A two-minute hand test settles it. The same gap affects "Sort saves automatically", which belongs to another feature: flagged here, not touched.

F2 · Notifications can start without the switch being turned on. High Verified in code, not seen on a device
notify_enabled defaults to true, and Settings registers the device whenever the phone has granted permission. Permission is granted by setting a reminder (RemindMe.tsx L53), and, on Android 12L and older, by default, because those versions have no runtime notification permission (Android's documentation; that expo then reports "granted" is Inferred). After either, simply opening Settings stores a token and "Saved" and "needs you" pushes begin, which contradicts the privacy policy's "off until you turn them on".

F3 · An import sends one notification per save. High Verified
Imported saves are inserted as pending with no push_sent_at (import-saves/index.ts L136–149), and a newly linked playlist's back catalogue arrives the same way; the sweeper then announces each one as it settles. On 15 September, 95 saves were claimed for a push in a single hour while the account had a live token. Whether those were delivered cannot be known, because nothing records sends. A 500-save import would mean hundreds of notifications within the hour (Inferred from the sweeper's batch of 50 per query, its 80-second budget and its five-minute rhythm).

F4 · "Saved" goes out when sorting failed or is waiting to retry. Medium Verified
After a failed attempt, runClassification still returns (with no category) (_shared/classification-worker.ts L35–52) and announce() runs regardless, because it reads only the save's link status. The one push is spent on "…is in your library", and the successful sort that follows tells no one. 24 saves have failed sorting; all were claimed.

F5 · The same save is announced two or three times. Medium Verified
A direct-message save gets Instagram's "Saved · Food" and a push seconds later. An iOS share-sheet save gets the extension's "Saved to Allkept" banner and then the push. A link pasted inside the app puts a banner over the screen the person is already looking at.

F6 · Signing out or deleting the account leaves reminders scheduled on the phone. Medium Verified in code
Neither sign-out (settings.tsx L114) nor deletion (lib/account.ts L17–28) cancels local notifications. A pending reminder still fires, showing a save's title to whoever holds the phone, until the next signed-in launch reconciles it away. This is the local-notification version of the bug claim_push_token() fixed for pushes.

F7 · Save titles appear on the lock screen. Medium Verified
Both pushes and reminders put the save's title in the body. Android's saves channel hides content on a secure lock screen, but reminders land in the fallback channel, which sets no private visibility (Inferred from the expo source). About one categorised save in six is about money, relationships, health or politics.

F8 · Delivery receipts are never read. Medium Verified
Only the immediate tickets are checked. Expo's documentation says delivery problems (a dead device, rate limits, bad credentials) show up in receipts fetched about fifteen minutes later. So dead tokens can linger and a credentials problem would be invisible.

F9 · No access token on sends. Medium Not verified
The sender sends no Authorization header (_shared/push.ts L58–62). Either Expo's optional "enhanced security" is off, in which case anyone who obtains a token can send to that phone, or it is on and every send is being refused. It is an EAS dashboard setting I could not see.

F10 · Device tokens go stale. Low Inferred
Registration is refreshed only while Settings is on screen, and a tab's screen is first drawn when it is first visited (React Navigation's default). The only token was last seen on 16 September. A reinstall or a rotated token is not repaired until Settings is opened.

F11 · "Needs you" means two things. Low Verified (queue item 19)
The push fires for preview_unavailable, where the person usually can do nothing; the Library's "needs attention" filter leaves that status out.

F12 · Nothing measures notifications. Medium Verified
There is no record of sends and no "opened from a notification" event. Because no save-level open is recorded, "you haven't opened this since…" cannot be said honestly today.

F13 · One Android channel for everything (account news included), created only when the switch is turned on. Low Verified

F14 · Cold-start routing could replay an old tap. Low Inferred
The last response is never cleared, so if the routing hook runs again (after signing out and back in, say), the old notification could open again.

F15 · The policy has drifted. Low Verified
§9 says permission is asked "not anywhere else"; reminders have asked since 15 September, and the policy is dated 14 September.

F16 · Deploy hazard. Medium Verified
Every function that would carry notification code (sweeper, reprocess-item, billing-webhook, weave) imports _shared/pipeline.ts, which holds another engineer's uncommitted Gemini work. Deploying from this working tree would ship that work (queue item 60).

Missing entirely

A send log; per-family preferences; a record of consent; quiet hours and the person's time zone; caps and back-off; delivery receipts; open measurement; routes for anything other than a save or the paywall; channels per family; any trip notification; one summary per batch; lock-screen-safe wording.

In plain English The pipes exist: phones register, the server can send, and taps open the right save. But the switch to turn notifications off probably does not work today, notifications can begin without anyone saying yes, an import of 93 saves triggered 95 "sent" marks in an hour, and the same save can be announced two or three times. Nothing records what was sent or opened. These need fixing before anything new is added.

2. Principles and global rules

2.1 The test every notification must pass

  1. Did the person start it, ask for it, or say yes to this kind? If not, don't send it.
  2. Is it about their own library, rather than about Allkept wanting attention?
  3. Is it true right now? Every send re-reads the state just before sending.
  4. Would they be glad of it even if they never tap it?
  5. Is it safe for whoever is holding the phone to read?

Only then do the mechanical gates apply: is this the only notification about this thing, is there budget left, is it outside quiet hours?

2.3 The weekly budget

  • At most 5 per person in any rolling 7 days, all kinds B, C and D together.
  • At most 2 of them C or D, at most one a day, never on two days running.
  • At most 2 a day of B, C and D together.
  • No C or D in the first 7 days after sign-up; let people find their feet.
  • Asked-for notifications (A) and account notices don't count: the person chose the first, and the second is rare and contractual.
  • When the budget is spent, C and D are dropped, never queued for later; B is folded into a roll-up ("3 saves need their links") or waits for the next allowed slot, and is always in the in-app list.

A typical week if everything here is built: one weekly note, nought or one "needs its link", and one or two trip notes in a week when a trip is being planned. That estimate is a judgement, not data.

2.4 Priority when several are due

  1. Account: a payment problem.
  2. Asked for: reminders and trip dates (local, at the person's time).
  3. A job finished: trip plan, saves read, import sorted.
  4. A save needs its link.
  5. The weekly note.
  6. A standalone old save (later).
  7. Away notes (only if ever built).

Two due within 30 minutes: send the higher one. If the lower is in the same family it is folded into the higher (one roll-up); otherwise B waits for the next slot and C or D is dropped.

2.5 Quiet hours and time zones

  • Quiet hours 21:00–08:00 in the person's own time for everything Allkept starts (B, C, D, account). What is due in the night waits until 08:00; C and D that miss their window that day are dropped rather than sent late.
  • One exception: when the person started the job less than 30 minutes ago (a plan begun at 23:00 that finishes at 23:04), it is sent at once: they are plainly awake and waiting.
  • Asked-for notifications (A) ignore quiet hours: the person chose the time.
  • Time zone: the server does not know it today. The phone should report its time-zone name (for example Asia/Kolkata) when the app starts and the zone has changed, the same pattern lib/language.ts uses for the language. Without a zone, nothing time-dependent (C, D) is sent to that person, and B follows the 30-minute rule only. Never guess the zone from the store country.
  • When travelling, the zone updates at the next launch; local notifications follow the phone's clock by themselves.
  • iOS Focus and Scheduled Summary are the person's own controls. Allkept never tries to break through them.

2.7 Back-off, dismissal and turning off

  • What "ignored" means: not opened within 48 hours. Swipes cannot be relied on: iOS reports a dismissal only for notifications set up with a custom dismiss action, and I am not certain expo-notifications reports dismissals on Android at all (Not verified). So the signal is "opened or not".
  • C (weekly note, old saves): two ignored in a row halves the rhythm (weekly becomes fortnightly). Four in a row pauses the family and puts a card in the app the next time the person opens it: "You haven't been opening the weekly note. Keep it, make it monthly, or switch it off?" No answer means it stays paused. Ask, don't assume.
  • B "needs its link": three ignored in a row stops those pushes; the saves wait in the in-app list, and Settings shows the switch as paused with a way to resume.
  • Away means no app opening and no new save for 14 days. Someone who keeps saving by direct message but never opens the app is not away: they are exactly who the weekly note is for. While someone is away only A and account notices continue, plus away notes if they were ever opted into; the rest resumes when they come back.
  • A family switched off: takes effect immediately, because preferences are read at the moment of sending, and the phone cancels that family's scheduled local notifications.
  • The master switch off: the device is forgotten (as today), the preference is actually saved (F1), and every local notification Allkept scheduled is cancelled. Reminders the person set themselves stay, because they asked for those, and Settings says so.
  • Turned off in the phone's own settings: noticed at the next launch, recorded, and sends stop. Allkept never nags; the existing "Allow them in Settings" row is the only way back.
  • In-app asks: "No thanks" is final for that ask. "Not now" waits 30 days. At most three asks for notification permission, ever.

2.8 Asking for permission

Keep the rule "never at first launch". Change "only from Settings" to "in context, at a moment that earns it". Allkept shows its own card first, and the phone's prompt appears only after the person taps "Turn on"; iOS gives an app a single chance at that prompt, so it should be spent on a yes. In order of strength:

  1. After "Make my plan". The wait is two to five minutes. "Want a notification when your plan is ready? You can leave this screen." [Notify me] [No thanks]. The value is obvious and immediate.
  2. When an import starts or a playlist is linked. "Sorting 93 saves takes a few minutes. Want a notification when they're done?"
  3. Setting a reminder, which asks today. Keep it, put one line of explanation before the phone's prompt, and make sure it no longer switches pushes on (F2).
  4. The Settings switch, as today.

A yes in context turns on the B families (needs its link, batches, trips). It never turns on C or D, which have their own asks. 2.9 iOS: "deliver quietly" and interruption levels

Provisional authorisation ("deliver quietly"). Apple lets an app start sending without asking. Recommendation: don't use it. It means sending to people who never said yes, which runs against "ask, don't assume" and against the privacy policy's "off until you turn them on". It would also change the share extension: that code treats quiet permission as permission (Swift L98) and would start posting its "Saved to Allkept" banner into Notification Center history. If reach ever matters more, use it for the weekly note alone, after the policy has been changed.

2.12 Lock screen and privacy

  • What lock-screen text may contain: counts, Allkept's own words, and the plain name of a built-in, non-sensitive category ("recipes", "places", "things to watch").
  • What it never contains unless Show save details is on: a save's title or author, a town or place, a person's name, an interest, the name of a custom category, or anything from Money & career, Life & relationships, Fitness & health or News & opinion. Travel plans are included in this, because a town on the lock screen tells a stranger you will be away from home.
  • What travels in the payload: ids only: the notification's id, its family, and the route to open.
  • Android: every channel private. iOS: whether a locked screen shows previews is the person's own setting; the wording must be safe even when it does.
  • Interest names never travel in a notification at all (see §3.6).

2.13 Grouping, the bell, badges

  • iOS threads per family (saves, trip-<id>, weekly, reminders, account), so Notification Center stacks them sensibly. Expo's push service supports a thread id.
  • Replace rather than stack: a trip's later stage replaces the earlier one ("saves read" becomes "plan ready"), and a roll-up replaces single notes. That is Expo's collapseId on iOS and its tag on Android.
  • The bell on Home opens a screen called "Notifications" that today lists recent saves. It should become the record of what Allkept actually told the person, read from the send log, so a notification swiped away by mistake can be found again (D16).
  • Badges: none. A number on the icon reads as a debt of unread saves, which is the opposite of calm. Keep shouldSetBadge: false.

2.14 How the words are written

  • Say what has happened, once it has happened. Re-check before sending.
  • Never blame the person, and never blame anyone else either: "arrived without its link", not "Instagram broke it", and never "check your connection" when the fault is ours.
  • No hype ("Don't miss", "Hurry", exclamation marks), no guilt ("You haven't…", "We miss you"), no streaks, no false urgency, no emoji.
  • Short: a lock screen shows about one line of each (the rule already in _shared/push.ts L121). Aim for a title under about 35 characters and a body under about 90.
  • One idea per notification, and the tap opens exactly the thing it names.
  • The same words on the lock screen as inside the app.
In plain English Allkept only speaks when the person started something, asked for something, or said yes to a kind of note. Five a week at most, two of them Allkept's own idea, none at night or in the first week, never the same thing twice, and quieter whenever they are ignored. Permission is asked at the moment its value is obvious, such as while a trip plan is being made, never at launch. The lock screen never gives away what someone saved.

  kind        text not null,     -- needs_link | batch_done | each_save | trip_read | trip_ready | trip_failed
                                 -- | weekly_note | rediscover | away | billing_ended | billing_issue
  dedupe_key  text not null,     -- names the thing (§2.6)
  status      text not null default 'queued',   -- queued | sent | skipped | failed
  skip_reason text,              -- paused | consent | muted | os_denied | stale | quiet | budget | backoff | no_device
  due_at      timestamptz not null default now(),
  retry_at    timestamptz,
  sent_at     timestamptz,
  opened_at   timestamptz,
  title text, body text,         -- exactly what was shown, for the in-app list; lock-safe unless show_details
  route       text,              -- what a tap opens, e.g. '/weave/plan?weaveId=…'
  tickets     jsonb,             -- Expo ticket ids per device, for receipts
  receipts_checked_at timestamptz,
  created_at  timestamptz not null default now(),

);
-- indexes: (status, due_at) where status = 'queued';  (user_id, sent_at desc) for budgets and the in-app list.
-- owner may read own rows; mark_notification_opened(id) sets opened_at; rows deleted after 90 days.

Local notifications (reminders, trip dates) are not in the log when they are scheduled; their taps are measured by the same notification_opened event. items.push_sent_at keeps being written until the log has replaced it, then stops.

4.5 How it is sent: keep Expo's push service

  • Why Expo, not Apple's and Google's services directly: it is already wired and tested (_shared/push.ts, tests/push.test.ts). The FCM credentials live in EAS, Expo handles APNs, and the stored tokens are Expo tokens. Going direct would mean keeping an APNs key and an FCM service account in Supabase's secrets, two code paths, and switching the app to native device tokens with a migration of the stored ones: more secrets and more work for no gain anyone would notice at this size. Expo's documented limits are 600 notifications per second per project and 4 KiB per message. Expo is already named in the privacy policy.
  • Add: the access token ("enhanced security"; closes F9); a receipts check 15 minutes to 24 hours after sending (Expo keeps receipts about a day), which marks dead devices and logs credential errors loudly (closes F8); and per message: channelId, interruptionLevel, threadId, collapseId or tag where one note replaces another, and ttl (trip ready 6 h, needs its link 24 h, weekly note 6 h: a stale note is worse than none). Passive notes use normal priority.

4.6 Words and languages

  • One catalogue of wording in packages/contracts, synced into the functions by scripts/sync-shared.mjs, so local and remote notifications use the same words. Each entry has an id, a family, a lock-safe title and body, a detailed variant, length limits and the route to open.
  • Tests: length limits; no title, town, name or interest in a lock-safe variant; a list of banned phrases; each claim tied to the state it describes.
  • English at launch. profiles.language already holds the phone's language (one of the three accounts has reported one), so translations can key off it later. Until then, English for everyone, rather than half-translated notes.

4.7 The gate

dispatch(row):
  if paused or family disabled in notification_config   → skip 'paused'
  s = settings(row.user)
  if s.consented_at is null                              → skip 'consent'   (closes F2)
  if family or kind switched off                         → skip 'muted'
  if s.os_permission = 'denied'                          → skip 'os_denied'
  if not still_true(row)                                 → skip 'stale'     (re-read save / trip)
  if quiet_hours(s) and not exempt(row)                  → B: due_at = next 08:00 · C/D: skip 'quiet'
  if over_budget(row.user, row.kind)                     → B: roll up or defer · C/D: skip 'budget'
  if backed_off(row.user, row.family)                    → skip 'backoff'
  words  = compose(row.kind, row.data, s.show_details, language)
  phones = live_tokens(row.user);  if none               → skip 'no_device'
  claim the row, send, store tickets, mark sent

4.8 When something fails

  • A notification never fails the thing that caused it (today's rule, kept).
  • The row is claimed before sending, so it is sent at most once. A passing failure (timeout, 429, a 5xx) gets a retry_at with back-off, up to the message's ttl, then is abandoned. A dead device is marked dead; a malformed message is logged.
  • Devices are registered again at every launch while consent stands (closes F10); devices dead for 30 days are deleted.
  • Sign-out and account deletion cancel this phone's scheduled notifications and clear the delivered ones (closes F6); the server already deletes the devices.

4.9 What travels, and what is kept

  • Payload: { nid, family, route }, plus the save or trip id where the route needs it. No content.
  • Title and body are lock-safe unless the person turns details on. Expo, Apple and Google see that text, as the policy already discloses, so it is kept minimal.
  • Function logs never contain a title or a body (today's don't).
  • The time zone is new personal data and needs a line in the policy. Interest names never travel. The log keeps the words for 90 days and is deleted with the account.

4.10 How it is measured

  • Server: the send log: sent, skipped by reason, failed, delivered (from receipts).
  • App (first-party app_events, as today): notification_opened {family, kind, nid}; notification_setting_changed {family, on, source}; notification_ask {moment, answer}; notification_permission {status} when it changes. The privacy policy describes a fixed list of events, so the list must be updated.
  • Per family: delivered → opened within 48 h → did the thing the notification was for → switched off within 7 days.
  • With one active account nothing here is statistically meaningful. For now these numbers exist to catch mistakes, such as a storm or a run of "consent" skips, not to tune open rates.

4.11 A pause switch

A one-row notification_config (paused; families enabled), read at sending. One SQL update stops everything or one family: no deploy, no build.

4.12 Deliberately not built

Pictures in notifications (iOS would need a Notification Service Extension, which means a native build); action buttons, except perhaps "Stop trip reminders" later; a notification vendor; send-time optimisation; A/B testing.

In plain English Every notification goes through one checkpoint that asks: did they say yes, is this switched on, is it still true, is it night where they are, have we already said enough this week? Then it sends and writes down what it sent. Keep using Expo to deliver, because it already works, and add the missing safety parts: a key on every send, delivery receipts, and a pause button that needs no deploy.

Appendix B · Outside sources checked

All read on 2 October 2026. Paraphrased, except the one short quotation in §3.2.

  • Apple, App Review Guidelines, 4.5.4 Push Notifications (developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines). Notifications must not be required for the app to work and should not carry sensitive personal information; promotions and direct marketing need explicit opt-in through consent words in the app and a way to opt out in the app. Related: 4.5.3 (no spam through Apple services) and 5.1.2(i) (an app may not require people to enable notifications to get something).
  • Apple, Human Interface Guidelines, Managing notifications (developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications). Get explicit permission before promotional notifications; let people manage notifications inside the app; Time Sensitive only for things happening now or within the hour, never for marketing; Passive for information people can read at leisure.
  • Apple, Asking permission to use notifications (developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications). Ask in context rather than at first launch. Provisional notifications are delivered quietly into Notification Center's history only, with Keep or Turn Off, and no prompt is shown.
  • Apple, UNNotificationInterruptionLevel (developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel). What Passive, Active, Time Sensitive and Critical each do.
  • Google Play, App Promotion (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899004). Forbids deceptive ads, including notifications that look like system notifications or alerts.
  • Google Play, Ads (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753). Ads must not imitate notifications or warnings of the operating system.
  • Google Play, Spam (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034). The rule I could confirm concerns messages sent on the user's behalf without confirmation. I found no explicit opt-in rule for promotional pushes on these three pages.
  • Android, Notification runtime permission (developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission). Android 13+ needs the runtime permission and new installs start with notifications off; Android 12L and lower need none; ask in context, after people understand the value.
  • Expo, Sending notifications (docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/sending-notifications). Message fields (including channelId, interruptionLevel, threadId, collapseId, tag, ttl, priority); tickets versus receipts (check receipts about 15 minutes later, kept about a day); 600 notifications per second per project; 4 KiB per message; an optional access token.
  • expo-notifications 57.0.17 source (in node_modules): the fallback channel "Miscellaneous" (BaseNotificationBuilder.kt, strings.xml); exact or inexact alarms (ExpoSchedulingDelegate.kt L106–114); the allowProvisional option; getLastNotificationResponseAsync deprecated and clearLastNotificationResponse available.
In plain English Apple is explicit: marketing notifications need a clear opt-in inside the app. Google's pages that I could read only forbid notifications that pretend to be system alerts, plus spam. Our own Terms are stricter than both, because they promise no marketing at all.
