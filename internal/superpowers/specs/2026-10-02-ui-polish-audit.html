Allkept — UI polish audit & a better “Plan a trip”

Phase 1: audit and design only. 2 October 2026 · iPhone 17 Pro simulator (402 × 874 pt), dev build, Pranav's real account · no source code changed.
In plain English — read this first

Plan a trip is broken today, and even when it works it feels fragile. On the simulator, “Read my saves” failed after 17 seconds and blamed Pranav's internet, which was fine. That needs a look at the server logs before anything else. Beyond that bug, the journey makes you stare at a spinner for minutes, throws away work you've paid for if you step away, loses your choices once a plan is made, and forgets the plan after an hour. Two controls also don't do what they say: the “nights” buttons get quietly re-balanced, and the date and time boxes open a keypad with no “-” or “:”, so on a real iPhone they can't be filled in the format the app demands (worth a 30-second check on a phone).

The rest of the app is close, but it isn't one system yet. The same jobs are built many different ways: 7 kinds of text box, 9 kinds of screen header, 6 kinds of bottom sheet, pills in 4 heights. Purple text is a bit too dim in dark mode, a long address can poke out of its pill, and the type scale has sizes but none of the spacing that makes text look finished.

What to do first: (1) find out why reading failed and stop blaming the person's connection; (2) two tiny, safe fixes — pills that can't overflow, and the full “Suggested — not from your saves” label; (3) make the date and time fields usable; (4) make reading and planning resumable, with a “Your trips” list so nothing paid for is lost; (5) then roll out the type scale and one shared text field, one screen at a time. Section 8 lists every step, one change each, for approval one by one.

The ten findings that matter most

  1. 1PT-01 — “Read my saves” failed within 17 s and told Pranav to check his connection while the phone was online — the journey can't currently be finished.
  2. 2PT-02 — Customise's date and time boxes open the iPhone number pad, which has no “-” or “:”; wrong entries are silently ignored, so plans quietly drop your dates.
  3. 3PT-03 — Reading waits up to 5 minutes behind a lone spinner; leaving the screen throws the paid read away.
  4. 4PT-04 / PT-05 — After a plan is made, Back loses your choices; failures offer only Back; plans disappear from the app after one hour.
  5. 5PT-06 — The “nights” buttons are quietly re-balanced when you tap 7 or 12 days — the plan doesn't use what the screen showed.
  6. 6AW-01 — A long address in the Maps pill spills out of its card (measured: 2.7 pt past the card edge, 19 pt past its padding).
  7. 7PT-08 — Suggested stops say only “Suggested”; the “not from your saves” words are missing on screen and dropped for VoiceOver.
  8. 8AW-02 / AW-03 — Dark mode's purple text and buttons fail contrast (3.94–4.45:1), and selected filters in dark mode nearly vanish (1.96:1).
  9. 9AW-04 / AW-05 — Fourteen text fields in seven styles; edges are 1.2:1 (Customise's are invisible) and none shows focus.
  10. 10AW-07 — Sheets with a text field (Done, Add place, New category) sit under the keyboard — likely hidden while typing (needs an on-device check).

1. Executive summary

In plain EnglishPlan a trip needs a working “read” step first, then a calmer, resumable journey. App-wide, the fix is fewer, shared building blocks (one text field, one header, one sheet, one chip) plus a proper type scale — done in small steps that can each ship over the air.

What's wrong

  • Plan a trip (20 findings: 2 critical, 6 high, 10 medium, 2 low). The read step failed live; the two minutes-long jobs have no real status, can't be resumed and are lost on Back; controls disagree with what is sent (nights, dates); errors offer no way forward; finished plans vanish after an hour; the plan itself is a long wall of text cards that look nothing like the rest of Allkept.
  • App-wide (29 findings: 7 high, 15 medium, 7 low). Duplicated primitives drifting apart (text fields, headers, sheets, pills, thumbnail rows, error messages); dark-mode accent contrast; near-invisible field edges and no focus state; a chip that can overflow; a type scale without line heights or tracking; press feedback, haptics and Reduce Motion handled differently in every component; small hit targets; misaligned Settings rows; sheets that likely sit under the keyboard.
  • What is already good and should be kept: the palette in light mode passes AA everywhere text matters (muted text 5.06–5.76:1); category tiles pass AAA in both themes; the flat category marks; the floating tab bar; ConfirmButton's reduced-motion-aware animation (the pattern to copy); Settings' honest copy; the Import screen's progress bar and “you can close the app” copy (the right model for long jobs).

What to do first

  1. Step 0 — investigate the failed read (server logs, no code).
  2. Steps 1–3 — Chip overflow, Chip's dropped accessibility label, the full “Suggested — not from your saves” tag. Each is a few lines in one file.
  3. Steps 4–6 — honest error copy with Try again; usable date/time pickers.
  4. Steps 10–13 — resumable reading and planning, “Your trips”, the JobCard.
  5. Steps 19–24 — type metrics and the shared TextField, then migrate one screen at a time.

2.4 The proposed journey

Design principles

  1. Asked for, never assumed. No place is ticked by the app; planning starts only from the person's tap. (Queued item 59 — a Home card asking “Planning a trip?” — can feed in later; not designed here as if it exists.)
  2. Jobs are first-class. Reading (~1–5 min) and planning (2–8 min) each get a JobCard: real steps, elapsed time, an honest range, “Do this later”, and Try again in place. Leaving never loses work: the weave is remembered the moment it starts and every screen can resume from the row.
  3. What you see is what is sent. Days and nights are one control that always adds up; the mix control has three clear states; dates and times come from pickers.
  4. Feels like Allkept. The person's own thumbnails during the wait and in the plan; the same chips, rows, sheets and type as the rest of the app; one primary action pinned at the bottom of every step.
  5. Two routes, both resumable. /weave?weaveId= (places → reading → profile) and /weave/plan?weaveId= (planning → plan). Pushed, never replaced, so Back always steps back one stage.

5. Typography system

In plain EnglishToday the app picks text sizes well but leaves the spacing between lines and letters to the font's defaults, which makes big text look loose and small text look cramped. The new scale fixes the line spacing and letter spacing for each size and adds the small sizes the app already needs, so every screen uses the same handful of styles.

Manrope ships here as static cuts (200–800) with no optical-size axis, and its built-in line height is 1.366 (hhea ascender 2132, descender 600, 2000 units/em — read from Manrope_400Regular.ttf). Its x-height is large (0.54 em), so it reads big at small sizes and loose at large ones. Optical sizing is therefore done by hand: tighter tracking and line height as size grows, looser tracking and a heavier weight as size shrinks. I measured label centring inside buttons and chips on the 3× screenshots: caps sit within 0.7 pt of centre, so no vertical-offset fix is needed.

Optical rules (Manrope, by hand)

  • Tracking: −2.4 % at 34 pt, −1.8 % at 28, −1.4 % at 22, −0.8 % at 18, 0 at 15–17, +1.2 % at 13, +5.8 % at 12 uppercase.

  const reduced = useReducedMotion();
