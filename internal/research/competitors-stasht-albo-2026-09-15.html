    Part 1 — Allkept against Stasht: where we stand and how we get ahead

    Sources: the two Stasht reports (product + feedback board), and the Allkept repository read directly (code, migrations, specs, forlater.md, SESSION_LOG.md). Every Allkept claim cites a path; every Stasht claim cites a report URL. "Unverified" means neither side's evidence settles it.

    1. Summary

    Stasht is ahead of Allkept as a shipped product and behind it as an engineered one. Stasht is on both stores (iOS since at least Mar 2026, Play since 2 Feb 2026), has a web app and Chrome/Safari extensions, bulk-imports from five platforms, and — its real pitch — turns saves into things: places on a map, events on a calendar, reminders, "nearby" alerts and weekly roundups, from captions, on-screen text and (it claims) speech (https://stasht.app/, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stasht-app-saves-that-work/id6756032175). It is free, and a native iOS 26 rewrite (v7.0.1) shipped today. Its traction is still tiny (27 App Store ratings, 500+ Play downloads, 231 Chrome users) and its own feedback board shows the cost of that breadth: ~20 posts about extraction getting it wrong, 7 about search, 6 about missing thumbnails, a confusing stashes/collections/AI-folders/tags/intent hierarchy, no way to override what the AI added, and 80 of 82 posts still "In Review" (https://stasht.featurebase.app/). Allkept is pre-launch (TestFlight build 25, launch date 21 Sep on the site, 3 profiles on 11 Sep — internal/store-submission.html, SESSION_LOG.md 11 Sep), mobile-only, and extracts far less per save (category, tags, one-line summary, typed entity names, an unused actionability — packages/contracts/src/index.ts): no map, no calendar, no reminders, no transcription. But on exactly the things Stasht's customers complain about, Allkept is structurally stronger today: one simple hierarchy (15 categories + categories you make) where your choice always wins and the model's answer is never overwritten (supabase/migrations/20260912210000_user_categories.sql, spec internal/superpowers/specs/2026-09-12-categories-tiles-and-user-categories-design.md), hybrid search (full-text + trigram + embeddings) over caption, note, summary, tags and entities (supabase/migrations/20260910030000_search_library_v2.sql), dedupe by platform identity, thumbnail retry engineering, a sorting switch and no forced translation, plus things Stasht has nothing like — an interests row computed from recurring entities, an Instagram DM door, YouTube playlist polling, in-app playback, and (as of today) a sorter that looks at the picture. The honest headline: Allkept is the better-built library; Stasht is the better after-save product; neither has users; Allkept charges in the US where Stasht is free. The race is open for about a year; Stasht's map/calendar extraction is the one thing their users love that Allkept cannot yet offer.

    3. Where Allkept is better today

    1. Override without erasure. item_ai.user_category wins everywhere and the model's category is kept; deleting a custom category returns saves to the model's choice, never to "Uncategorized" (migrations/20260912210000_user_categories.sql; spec §Part 2). Stasht's top-voted post says "I can't control what is added, why, etc." (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/first-days-with-stasht) and "Category addition" has sat unanswered since Sep 2025 (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/category-addition).
    2. One hierarchy. A save sits on exactly one shelf; interests are computed, not filed (internal/superpowers/specs/2026-09-13-explore-interests-design.md). Stasht has stashes vs collections vs AI folders vs tags vs Type/Intent, which produced "What is the purpose of collections?" (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/purpose-of-collections), "Simplify the sorting" and the 12-videos-shows-6 bug.
    3. Search that ranks by meaning. Four-tier scoring ending in embedding distance under 0.55 (migrations/20260910030000_search_library_v2.sql lines 33–52), scoped by RLS as the caller (supabase/functions/search-library/index.ts), with a cached query embedder. Stasht's search is keyword over enriched text, semantic unverified, and is its second-largest complaint cluster.
    4. Duplicates are folded, not shown twice. Identity = platform id or canonical URL; a duplicate found after short-link expansion is folded into the original with the capture re-pointed (_shared/duplicate.ts, _shared/pipeline.ts 23505 branch); the library flags "Saved more than once" (lib/filter-options.ts). Stasht users report duplicate events and posts (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/duplicate-events).
    5. Thumbnail reliability is engineered. Retry ladder for walled pages, poster fallback when the held address is a video file, phone-side backfill through a per-platform picture door, "sweeper pass 4" for settled cards (SESSION_LOG.md 14 Sep; _shared/picture-hosts.ts; lib/instagram-picture.ts). Stasht has six "media missing / thumbnail not showing" posts across a year (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/media-missing).
    6. Consent and opt-outs exist. "Sort saves automatically" can be turned off and then nothing is sent to a model (_shared/pipeline.ts checks ai_sorting_enabled server-side); interests ask before showing (profiles.interests_enabled null until asked); summaries are written in the caption's language, never translated (_shared/classify.ts). Stasht drew complaints for forced translation and junk enrichment with no opt-out (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/first-days-with-stasht).
    7. Interests. user_interests() counts recurring entities across the library with a floor of three, recency weighting and an icon per name (migrations/20260913090000_interests.sql, 20260915090000_interest_icons.sql, lib/interests.ts). Nothing on Stasht's site, store pages or board resembles this; their closest request is "Auto tagging" with folder summaries (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/auto-tagging).
    8. Hands-free doors. Instagram DM with a reply carrying the category (supabase/functions/instagram-webhook/index.ts), YouTube playlist polling with a pause-and-reason on the paywall (forlater.md #14 T3). Stasht has no DM/bot path anywhere (product report §2, absence noted).
    9. Picture-aware sorting since today. The classifier receives the stored poster frame as bytes from the private bucket (_shared/pipeline.ts pictureForModel; spec 2026-09-15-classify-with-the-picture-design.md), which directly targets the "vibe caption, wrong category" failure Stasht users hit (workout filed as Travel).
    10. Destructive actions confirm. Delete asks first (components/ItemDetail.tsx confirmDelete); sign-out and account deletion do too. Stasht deleted a post when a user pressed Cancel (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/deleted-post-when-i-hit-cancel).
    11. Ships on older iPhones. Stasht v7 requires iOS 26 (App Store listing); Allkept's share target deploys to 15.1 (specs/2026-09-11-share-extension-save-design.md).

    4. Where Allkept is worse today — bluntly

    1. Nothing happens after the save except a label. No map, calendar, reminders, nearby alerts or weekly roundup. Push fires only on "sorted" or "attention" (_shared/push.ts). ACTIONABILITY (watch/try/buy/go/read) is extracted and stored but no app code reads it (grep of apps/mobile found zero uses). Stasht's users open the app because of what it brings back; Allkept has no reason-to-return loop, and cannot even measure returns (forlater.md #20).
    2. No transcription or OCR. Enrichment is oEmbed + Open Graph + one poster frame (_shared/enrich.ts). A recipe reel with a thin caption is nearly invisible to Allkept's search; Stasht reads speech and on-screen text (their claim; a user confirms "It does a good job of taking information from the videos"). docs/competitive-landscape.html already names this the largest gap.
    3. Entities are names, not things. No geocoding, hours, prices, dates (ItemAiOutput in packages/contracts/src/index.ts). Stasht's public page shows nine venues with ratings, addresses and hours from one video.
    4. No desktop, no web, no extensions, thin import. Import is the Instagram export only (supabase/functions/import-saves/index.ts, _shared/saved-export.ts); YouTube needs an unlisted playlist (app/setup/youtube.tsx); no TikTok/X/Reddit/Pinterest import (internal/tiktok-plan.html, internal/whatsapp-x-plan.html are plans). Stasht bulk-imports from five platforms plus X sync — flaky (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/problem-with-chrome-extension) but real.
    5. Not launched, and the two flagship doors are gated. DM saves work only for Meta testers until App Review (forlater.md #8; ~20-day clock from 11 Sep); the silent share extension is committed but no build has been made (forlater.md #18, on hold); store billing is blocked on an App Store Connect ticket and a Google merchant profile (forlater.md #14). Stasht has shipped for seven-plus months.
    6. Charging where the competitor is free. $2.99/mo after 25 saves in the US (app/subscribe.tsx, lib/paywall.ts). Category-wide subscription resistance is documented in docs/competitive-landscape.html, and a Stasht reviewer explicitly contrasts it with subscription apps. Also a latent trap: any storefront that is neither IN nor null (UK, CA…) is blocked after 25 with nothing to buy (entitled(); lib/standing.ts line 68–74) — fine for a US+India launch, wrong the day availability widens.
    7. Fewer knobs. No tag editing (tags are display-only in ItemDetail.tsx), no multi-select or bulk move, no done/visited state, no per-save reminder, no collections or sharing beyond the OS sheet. Stasht shipped Faves/Done today and has tags, notes, reminders and public collections.
    8. Custom categories do not teach the model. The classifier keeps choosing from the fixed fifteen; new saves land in a built-in until moved (spec 2026-09-12-categories-tiles-… "Not doing"). The app copy says so, but it is the same gap Stasht's "Category addition" request describes.
    9. Scale is untested. Largest library in the database is ~159 saves (specs/2026-09-15-classify-with-the-picture-design.md). Pagination (30/page, lib/library.ts) and FlashList exist, and the sweeper's pass-4 has no index (forlater.md #37). Stasht broke at 200+ items in a collection; Allkept has never had 200.
    10. Platform-terms exposure is shared but unresolved. Instagram thumbnails/captions come from fetching the page's OG tags, which Meta's Automated Data Collection Terms name (SESSION_LOG.md 15 Sep, 4th revision; _shared/enrich.ts line 310 comment). Stasht re-hosts the video itself, which is arguably worse, but Allkept has not had the counsel hour either.

    6. How Allkept gets better than Stasht — ranked moves

    Sizes are relative to this codebase (small ≈ a day or two, medium ≈ a week, large ≈ several weeks plus native builds).

    Fix what we have

    1. Ship the silent share extension, then the "Saved — filed under Food. Change?" sheet on next open (route B). Code is committed (forlater.md #18, #31); needs EAS builds and one React sheet. Beats Stasht: their share flow shows a loading ring users misread (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/get-rid-of-timer) and offers no override at all. Size: small (builds + one sheet). Proves: capture friction is solved and every automatic decision has a two-tap correction — the single most repeated need on their board.
    2. Surface actionability and add a Done flag. actionability is already stored per save and unused; add it as a filter group ("To go / To try / To buy / To watch") and a done flag next to noted/repeated (lib/filter-groups.ts, library_query_v3). Beats Stasht: their "Visited" is High Priority and unanswered (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/visited); their intent filter exists but adds to the confusion. Size: small. Proves: intent-based retrieval with zero new AI cost.
    3. Resurfacing: record opens, build the three home sections, send a weekly digest push. forlater.md #20 and #22 plus one cron using the existing push path (_shared/push.ts). Beats Stasht: weekly summaries are their most-praised feature (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/location-based-notifications); Allkept can do it per interest ("three new saves about Julian Goldie this week"), which they cannot. Size: medium. Proves: retrieval rate — the metric docs/competitive-landscape.html says decides the category.
    4. Editable tags + multi-select bulk move/delete. Tags are display-only in ItemDetail.tsx; add a selection mode to library.tsx with a batch RPC. Beats Stasht: bulk edit is their #3 request and lives only in an unreleased V2 beta (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/bulk-tag-editmove-or-collection-merge). Size: medium. Proves: the library scales to hundreds without a second hierarchy.
    5. Density: grid/list toggle and compact cards. Beats Stasht: High Priority, unanswered (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/smaller-thumbnails-and-choice-between-grid-and-list). Size: small. Proves: glanceability at 200+ saves.

    Build what they lack

    1. "Why this category" with confidence, and a one-tap fix that teaches. Show the stored confidence and summary as the reason; add a rule engine ("everything from @account → Wedding") the categories spec already names as the right shape, and feed the person's own category names into the prompt only when a rule exists. Beats Stasht: their top complaint is opacity ("I can't control what is added, why") and their custom-category request is unanswered. Size: medium. Proves: the AI gets better for a person over time, which no rival on the board does.
    2. Place list before map: geocode place entities, list by city, manual add/edit. A places provider with per-place caching is already costed in internal/compile-economics.html. Do the list view + override first, map second and never on Home. Beats Stasht: three separate posts ask for a list by city, not pins (https://stasht.featurebase.app/p/map, …/search-bar, …/bad-results); manual location edit is High Priority unanswered; their map annoys recipe savers. Size: medium-large. Proves: the "after the save" value on the highest-intent category without inheriting their wrong-state pin bug.
    3. Make the paid tier buy something Stasht cannot give: Weave. Itinerary / cook-this-week / watchlist artefacts grounded in the person's saves (forlater.md #34, internal/compile-economics.html). Beats Stasht: they organise; nobody on the board asks for or gets a composed output, and their terms only reserve "premium features". Size: large. Proves: a reason to pay that survives a free competitor.

    Do what they do that we don't

    1. Transcription and on-screen text into the search document and the classifier. Largest functional gap (docs/competitive-landscape.html §"Behind on"); the picture path built today (_shared/pipeline.ts pictureForModel) is the pattern to extend. Beats Stasht: their search over transcripts ranks badly; Allkept's ranking is better and would finally have the text. It also multiplies interests, because speech names more entities than captions. Size: large (media access, cost per save rises well above $0.005 — internal/unit-economics.html). Proves: "gochujang finds the recipe nobody typed" with correct results.
    2. Dates and events, editable from day one.     8. Risks — where Stasht could overtake, and what to watch

    • Free plus native. If they ship overrides and a semantic search, Allkept's engineering edge halves while their after-save lead stays.

    • Allkept's doors depend on Meta. DM capture and export import are surfaces Meta can withdraw; the OG page fetch is the first counsel question (SESSION_LOG.md 15 Sep). Stasht has the same exposure plus video re-hosting, but a Meta action would hit both, and only the share sheet survives it.
    • Pricing. $2.99/mo against a free rival that a reviewer already praises for being free; and the non-US/non-IN storefront trap in entitled().
    • AI sorting is becoming table stakes (Apple's iOS 27 Safari auto-organisation, SESSION_LOG.md 15 Sep).

    7. Underlying themes

    1. Trust in automatic extraction. Users like that Stasht pulls places, events, recipes, and categories out of a reel, but every failure (wrong state for Target, Halloween as Christmas, workout as travel, "All Day" events, duplicates, Chinese junk links, forced translation) erodes it. Evidence: ~20 bug posts, the top-voted "First days" post, Make events editable (2), Venues with Multiple locations. The requester's words: "I can't control what is added, why."
    2. Manual control as the safety valve. The most recent High-Priority requests are all overrides: edit/delete events, add/edit locations, custom categories that persist, subfolders, bulk tag/move/merge, a "not in any collection" filter, visited checkmark. People accept imperfect AI if they can fix it in two taps.
    3. Search is the primary retrieval path and it is not reliable. "Move search to be first" was the first thing shipped; 7 later posts say search returns wrong or missing results, especially geographic queries ("NJ bookstores", "restaurants by city").
    4. A confusing information architecture. Stashes vs collections vs AI-generated folders vs tags vs categories vs "Type/Intent" sorting. Direct evidence: "What is the purpose of collections? Why not just put everything under stashes?", "Simplify the sorting", the 12-videos-shows-6 bug caused by auto-folders, tag creation mistaken for search, "+" vs tap on suggested tags.
    5. Recipes and places are the two killer use cases. Recipe savers (Japanese reels, bread, carbonara tips, "all my recipes categorized as other") and travellers ("on the road a lot", map by city, visited, offline mode, distance in miles, location notifications). The map matters to travellers and is clutter to recipe people ("I do not need the map thing… it takes a lot of place on the screen").
    6. Performance is the first thing power users hit. 200+ item collections lag, slow reel loading, missing thumbnails, iPad loading. The team's own admission: scaling work after a user influx.
    7. Getting existing saves in, from everywhere. Bulk import from IG/YouTube, Chrome extension failures across five networks, Facebook, Resy, GIFs, Gemini links, two accounts. The import feature existed but a user with 2 votes didn't know — discoverability, not capability.
    8. Density and glanceability. Smaller thumbnails, grid/list toggle, fewer sort options, no loading ring after share.

    8. Lessons for a competitor building the same product

    Make sure we do:

    • Ship an override for every automatic decision (category, location, event date/time, entity type, tags) and make deleting a wrong event/duplicate trivial. This is the single most repeated need on the board (themes 1–2).
    • Treat search as the core feature: full-text over captions + transcripts + extracted entities, with place/city filtering. The city-search-returns-list request appears three times (Map, Search bar, Bad results).
    • Keep the hierarchy simple and explainable. One container concept with optional nesting (the Raindrop comparison was explicit) beats stashes + collections + AI folders + tags.
    • Bulk operations from day one: multi-select, move, tag, merge collections, "uncategorised" filter. Stasht only offers this in an unreleased V2 beta.
    • Performance budget for 200+ item collections and thumbnail reliability; Stasht's thumbnail bugs are 6 posts across a year. Cache the thumbnail at save time and show a fallback when the source is deleted (users asked what happens when a reel is removed).
    • Import discoverability: if we have IG/YouTube import, surface it in onboarding; Stasht's user asked for a feature that already existed.
    • Confirm destructive actions properly and never let "Cancel" delete (Deleted Post When I Hit Cancel).
    • Close the loop publicly: mark things Completed, keep a changelog. Stasht's fast fixes are invisible on its own board.

    Avoid:

    • Auto-translating or auto-enriching without an opt-out; both drew complaints on the top-voted post.
    • Creating AI-generated folders that fragment a user's collection (the 12-shows-6 bug).
    • A share-sheet loading ring that implies the user must wait.
    • Forcing the map onto the home screen for users who save recipes, not places.
    • Metric units for a US audience (small, but noticed).

    Openings (unmet on Stasht as of 2026-09-13):

    • Nested collections / subfolders (High Priority, unanswered).
    • Manual location add/edit and "visited" state for places (both High Priority, unanswered).
    • Editable/deletable calendar events with correct times.
    • Grid/list toggle and compact thumbnails.
    • Offline cache for travellers.
    • Import beyond IG saves and YouTube Watch Later (arbitrary YouTube playlists, TikTok favourites, X bookmarks, Reddit saved — Stasht's extension failed on all of these for one user).
    • GIF/meme collections; Facebook posts; reservation-app shares (Resy).
    • A "why was this added" explanation for each auto-extracted item, since the top post's core complaint is opacity.

    Part 5 — Albo, the product and its customers

    Summary. Albo ("Albo: Save, Organise & Plan", formerly "Sortd", bundle ID co.uk.isaacng.backburner) is a London-built universal save-for-later app from The Feel Good Project Ltd, founded by Isaac Ng, that pitches itself as helping you "Actually do the things you save". You save via the system share sheet (or paste a link, or bulk-import) from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, Pinterest, Reddit, LinkedIn, Threads, Facebook, Safari/Chrome and screenshots; an AI pipeline "watches" the video/post and extracts a typed record (recipe with ingredients and steps, place with address/hours pinned to a map, product with price, event with date and "Add to Calendar", film/book/game/album, article summary, workout, software), auto-categorises it, and lets you organise into collections, collection folders, ranked lists, smart/filtered views and custom AI-built "extract types". On top sit a map (Apple or Google view, friends' pins, trending spots, events), a chat assistant ("Ask Albo", on-device option, Siri, a Claude MCP integration), reminders/resurfacing, a weekly recap, a trip planner, a meal planner/grocery list, and a social layer (friends, shared collections with chat and voting, community/nearby feeds, journal reviews). It launched on the App Store on 16 May 2025 (as Sortd), renamed to Albo around v1.0 in Oct 2025, added Android on 13 Dec 2025, and ships almost weekly (v1.22.0 on 14 Sep 2026 with iOS 27 support).
