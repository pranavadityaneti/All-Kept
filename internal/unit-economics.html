Allkept — unit economics

12 September 2026 · costs per platform, per user, per save and per AI read. Figures marked measured come from the live database; unverified means I could not confirm it against a vendor's own page and it should be checked before it is relied on.

The short version

$0.0051 per save
That is the whole marginal cost of a save today, and roughly 99% of it is one AI call. Storage, database, bandwidth and function invocations are rounding errors beside it.
In plain English. Every time somebody saves something, it costs you about half a cent — and almost all of that is asking the model to sort it. Nothing else you are paying for scales with usage in a way that matters yet. So the only number worth managing is the cost of one sorting call, and the only question that matters is who is making those calls for free.

Per platform — it barely matters

The obvious guess is that a long Reddit post costs more to sort than a bare Instagram link. Measured, it does not:

The spread is ±10% — Instagram is the dearest only because its captions are the longest. Platform is not a cost lever. What differs between platforms is work that is free: which endpoint is fetched, and how often it refuses.

In plain English. It costs you the same to keep a TikTok as a Reddit post. Do not pick which platforms to support on cost grounds — pick on whether the door works and whether people use it.

The number that should decide something

India is free, and free users cost the same as paid ones. The cost follows engagement, not revenue, and there is nothing between a keen user and your bill.

10,000 Indian users at 100 saves/monthMonthly costYearly
Current model — gpt-5.6-sol$5,096$61,000
Bulk model — gpt-5.6-terra$2,806$34,000
Cheapest — gpt-5.6-luna$281$3,400

Same product, same behaviour, an 18× spread depending only on which model sorts a free user's save.

In plain English. Ten thousand enthusiastic Indian users could cost you sixty thousand dollars a year, or three thousand, and the difference is one setting. That is worth a deliberate decision rather than a default.

The levers, in the order I would pull them

  1. A cheaper model for the free tier. The machinery already exists — classifiers.ts has a bulk tier (BULK_MODEL = gpt-5.6-terra) used for imported back catalogues, chosen per call. Routing free-tier saves through a cheaper tier is a configuration change, not a rebuild. What it costs you: sorting quality, which nobody has measured. Worth an honest A/B on real saves before committing.
  2. Shorten the input. 629 input tokens carries the prompt plus the save's text. The prompt is paid for on every single save; trimming it is a permanent saving with no quality cost, and caching it would cut the input charge tenfold on vendors that support it.
  3. Do not sort twice. "Sort again" re-runs a full call, and a failed classification retries up to five times. Both are correct behaviours, both cost again — worth a cap per item per period.
  4. A monthly ceiling for free accounts. Unpopular, but it turns an unbounded liability into a known one.

What would change these numbers

  • X bookmarks would be the first door with a real per-use price: $0.005 per Post read, roughly $2.50 for a typical import — per user, on a free action. That is as much as a whole month of sorting for that person. See whatsapp-x-plan.html.
  • WhatsApp would add none: inbound messages and in-window replies are free.
  • Anything on the home screen that spends an AI call — "ask your saves", smarter resurfacing — converts a per-save cost into a per-visit one, which is a different and worse shape. The home-screen plan flags this.

What I could not verify

  • The embedding price. Immaterial at any plausible value, but it is from memory.
  • Which Supabase plan the project is on, and therefore whether storage, egress and invocations are billed at all today.
  • EAS build allowance and whether this week's builds have exceeded it.
  • Real usage. Everything per-user here is a rate card against assumed behaviour; one test account is not data.
