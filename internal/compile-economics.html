In plain English. A save costs half a cent to sort. Turning a hundred of them into a trip plan costs about the price of a small coffee's tax — twenty cents on the best model, seven on the next one down — plus whatever we pay to look up where each place actually is. The model is not the expensive part of an itinerary; the places lookup can be, and it is the line to design around. Everything else on the list is cheaper than travel, and two categories need no model at all. A person who uses this heavily for a month costs about a dollar. That is a paid feature with room in it.

The rule that keeps input small: send the record, not the caption, except where the caption is the point. The sorting already distilled every save into 84 tokens; a Make reads the distillate.

Place lookups — 60 places unverified—geocoding alone is a few tenths of a cent a place; details with hours are historically around a cent or two. Uncached, this line can exceed the model. Cached per place and shared across everyone, it collapses: Blue Bottle Kyoto is looked up once, ever.

What makes it realistic is not the model size but the grounding: only places that appear in the saves; the facts from the lookup, not from memory; the plan composed by area and pace; every stop cited to the reel it came from; anything assumed marked as a suggestion. That discipline costs nothing extra in tokens and is the difference between a plan and a plausible paragraph.

Ask your saves — one question

The general form of all of the above: a question over the library, answered from the ten most relevant saves (the search embeddings already exist). About 1,200 tokens in and 300 out: $0.014 on Opus 5, $0.006 on Sonnet 5. Twenty questions a month is $0.29 on Opus.

What moves the number

LeverEffect
Send records, not captionsThe single biggest saving, already built into the numbers above: 100 tokens a save instead of the ~630 sorting reads. Only Food needs captions.
Cache the instructions1,800 tokens at a tenth of the price on every call after the first. Regenerating the same Make within the cache window also re-reads the save records at a tenth.
Output lengthOutput is five times the price of input. An itinerary at 4,000 tokens is half its own cost; a tighter format (stops as short lines, detail on tap) halves the bill.
Batch, when nobody is waitingHalf price. "Your Kyoto plan will be ready in a minute" is an honest experience and cuts every number above in two; keep the interactive path for a person who is watching.
Sonnet for the plain listsWatchlists, checklists and routines do not need the best model; Opus earns its price on the itinerary, where composition and judgement show. Two models, chosen per category.
Cache place lookups per placeThe one line that can outgrow the model. Look a place up once, keep it, share it across every person who saves it.
No model where none is neededA mood board is thumbnails and a palette, assembled on the phone. A meme digest is a list.

What to verify before relying on this

  • Output sizes are assumed. Build one itinerary as a prototype and count its tokens with the API's count endpoint — the whole table scales with that one number.
  • Characters to tokens at 4:1 holds for English. Captions in Hindi, Telugu or Japanese tokenise at roughly twice the density; a library in those languages costs more per save than measured here.
  • Place lookup pricing is the unverified line and the one that can dominate. Choose the provider, read its sheet, and decide whether hours are worth paying for or whether coordinates and area are enough for a first version.
  • No travel saves exist yet in the database to measure against; Travel is modelled on the all-category averages plus place facts.
  • Prices: Anthropic's from the price sheet cached 24 June 2026 (Opus 5 $5/$25, Sonnet 5 $2/$10 per million; cache reads a tenth); gpt-5.6-sol at $4/$20 as checked on 8 September.
