Outcome (12 Sep): the card route below was built and shown; on iOS 26 the sheet container stayed opaque and full height even under NSExtensionActionWantsFullScreenPresentation (Apple forums 806121). Pranav chose no card at all: the extension queues the link, hands the upload to a background URL session and closes at once — the sheet "vanished in a flash", and the save reached the server two seconds later. A small card remains only for "Open Allkept to sign in" and "That wasn't a link". Kept as the record of the research.

Why the share extension shows a full black sheet, what the well-regarded apps do instead, the route Apple allows, and the card I propose. Written 12 September 2026.

1. What you saw, and why

iOS presents every share extension inside a system sheet fixed at full height on iPhone. Our extension's view is transparent, so the sheet shows its own dark surface with the banner floating in the middle — the black sheet in your screenshot. Nothing in the extension can shrink that sheet: Apple's Frameworks Engineer, answering exactly this question on the developer forums, said the detents are not customisable and that the alternative is a plist key that removes the sheet entirely:

"Are you asking if there is a way to customize the detents on the UISheetPresentationController instance used to present your extension? If so, unfortunately the answer is no … Alternatively, you can specify NSExtensionActionWantsFullScreenPresentation in your extension's Info.plist, which will present your extension over the full screen with no additional chrome (e.g. the shadow, dimming, or corner radius provided by UISheetPresentationController). Then you can size your extension however you want within that full screen view. However, this is not recommended, as this approach loses the standard look and pull to dismiss behavior."
— Apple Frameworks Engineer, developer.apple.com/forums/thread/694643

The "not recommended" caveat is about losing pull-to-dismiss. A card that closes itself after a second has nothing to pull down, so the caveat does not apply to us. This is the same mechanism the banner-style extensions below use.

Specification

AspectDecision
PresentationNSExtensionActionWantsFullScreenPresentation = true; the root view is fully transparent, so the app you shared from stays visible and undimmed. No sheet, no grabber.
CardBottom-anchored, 16 pt from the side edges, sitting above the home indicator; corner radius 18; the app icon at 28 pt on the left; title in semibold 15 pt; an optional second line in 13 pt at 70 % opacity (only the offline message uses it). Width fits the text, capped at the screen width minus 32.
ColoursFollows the system appearance: near-black at 94 % opacity with white text in dark mode; near-white at 96 % with ink text in light mode. Uses the brand purple only in the icon.
MotionSlides up 12 pt and fades in over 180 ms while the request is in flight (shows "Saving…"); the text swaps in place to the result; holds 1.1 s; fades out over 200 ms; then the extension completes. Total on screen ≈ 1.5 s online.

TapTapping anywhere dismisses immediately (the whole transparent view is the hit area). Nothing else is interactive.
AccessibilityThe result is posted as a VoiceOver announcement; text scales with Dynamic Type up to the accessibility sizes, wrapping to two lines.
AndroidAlready a toast over the host app (proven on the emulator). Unchanged.

4. The exact change

  • targets/share/Info.plist: add NSExtensionActionWantsFullScreenPresentation = true under NSExtensionAttributes.
  • targets/share/ShareViewController.swift: root view .clear; replace the centred label with a bottom-anchored card view (icon + title + optional subtitle), the fade/slide animations, the tap-to-dismiss recogniser, the VoiceOver announcement. The request logic, the queue and the messages are untouched.
  • Local rebuild (unsigned simulator build, ~6 min) → you tap Share → Allkept once more and see the card over Safari.
  • Then the EAS preview builds.
One thing to verify on the first run: that with the full-screen presentation the app underneath is actually visible through our transparent view on iOS 26 — the forum answer says the presentation has no dimming or chrome, and Instapaper's banner relies on exactly that, but I have not seen it on this OS version myself. If it turns out black, the fallback is a translucent dark scrim behind the card, which still beats today's sheet.
