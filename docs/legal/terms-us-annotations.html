2. Cross-cutting: how the terms become binding

2.1 Online assent

VERIFIED Chabolla v. ClassPass, Inc., No. 23-15999 (9th Cir. Feb. 27, 2025), cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/…/23-15999.pdf: a "sign-in wrap" is enforceable only if the site gives reasonably conspicuous notice of the terms and the user takes an action that unambiguously manifests assent (following Berman v. Freedom Financial Network and Oberstein v. Live Nation). ClassPass lost because the notice on its screens was not conspicuous enough. The court looked at font size, contrast, placement near the action button, and whether hyperlinks looked like links.

VERIFIED Douglas v. U.S. District Court (Talk America), 495 F.3d 1062 (9th Cir. July 18, 2007), caselaw.findlaw.com/…/1307125.html: "Parties to a contract have no obligation to check the terms on a periodic basis to learn whether they have been changed by the other side"; a revised contract posted without notice "is merely an offer and does not bind the parties until it is accepted", and an arbitration clause, class waiver and choice-of-law clause added that way did not bind the customer.

VERIFIED E-SIGN, 15 U.S.C. §7001(a), law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001: a contract "may not be denied legal effect, validity, or enforceability solely because it is in electronic form". §2.1 of the draft records the electronic-contract consent.

Product requirements that follow (not in the terms themselves). (1) The sign-in and account-creation screens must show, in readable type next to the button, "By continuing you agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy", with the two names as visibly styled links that open the documents; a checkbox is safer still. (2) The screen should call out the arbitration clause and opt-out in one line, because the arbitration agreement is the term most often attacked for lack of notice. (3) Existing users must be shown the new US terms in-app and asked to accept (or be given 30 days' notice and the opt-out), not merely have them posted — Douglas. (4) The app must record the date and version accepted per account, so the 30-day opt-out window in §19.11 can be proven. DECISION These are build items for the store-submission release.

2.2 New Jersey TCCWNA — drafting rule applied everywhere

SECONDARY N.J.S.A. 56:12-15 to -17. The primary text could not be fetched today (justia, FindLaw, public.law, onecle and njcourts.gov all refused or 404'd). The wording is taken from a Hunton Andrews Kurth client alert of April 2018, hunton.com/…/36846_new-jersey-supreme-court-clarifies-truth-in-consumer-contract.pdf, which quotes the statute: a consumer contract may not include "any provision that violates any clearly established legal right of a consumer or responsibility of a seller" (§56:12-15); may not state "that any of its provisions is or may be void, unenforceable or inapplicable in some jurisdictions without specifying which provisions are or are not void, unenforceable or inapplicable within the State of New Jersey" (§56:12-16); and an "aggrieved consumer" may recover "$100.00 or [] actual damages, or both … together with reasonable attorney's fees and court costs" (§56:12-17). Spade v. Select Comfort Corp. (N.J. Apr. 16, 2018), summarised in the same alert and in the search results, held that an "aggrieved consumer" is one "who has been harmed by a violation" — which reduced, but did not remove, the litigation risk from website terms.

Drafting choice. No clause anywhere in the draft says a provision "may be void in some jurisdictions". Where a carve-out depends on state law, the draft says "if you live in a state that…" and then §21.2 states, for New Jersey specifically, which provisions apply and which do not. The jury-trial waiver that most US terms carry was deliberately omitted: it adds little once arbitration exists, pre-dispute jury waivers are not enforced in California (UNVERIFIED — Grafton Partners v. Superior Court (Cal. 2005), not fetched), and a flat waiver is exactly the kind of clause a TCCWNA plaintiff targets. DECISION Counsel should confirm the New Jersey paragraph against the current statute and the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act, Products Liability Act and Punitive Damages Act, whose section numbers are deliberately not cited in the draft because none was fetched.

3. §1 Who we are and what these terms cover

Legal basis. Identification of the contracting party, its address and a contact is required by California Civil Code §1789.3(b) for providers of an "electronic commercial service" (VERIFIED, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov … CIV 1789.3) and by Apple's Minimum Terms item "Developer Name and Address" (VERIFIED, apple.com/legal/…/minterms).

Drafting choice. Scope is tied to where the user lives or which storefront they used, so the India and US documents cannot both apply to one person. The Privacy Policy is incorporated because the India terms already do that and because the US commitments in §12 (no ads, no sale) are meant to be contractual.

Alternative considered. Many US services state that the privacy policy is not part of the contract (to keep policy changes out of contract claims). Rejected: the founder's position is that "no ads, no sale" is a promise, and making it enforceable is the point.

4. §2 Acceptance and eligibility

Legal basis. Assent and E-SIGN as in section 2. Age: COPPA applies to operators that collect personal information from a "child", defined as "an individual under the age of 13" (VERIFIED, 15 U.S.C. §6501(1), law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/6501; §6502(a)(1), law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/6502). The FTC's COPPA Rule page shows the amended Rule published in the Federal Register on April 22, 2025 (VERIFIED, ftc.gov/…/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa); the eCFR text of 16 CFR Part 312 could not be fetched (redirect to a bot-check page), so the effective and compliance dates of the 2025 amendments are UNVERIFIED.

Drafting choice. 18+ is the founder's product decision and matches both store age ratings. The draft states the 18+ rule, and separately that no data is knowingly collected from under-13s, so the COPPA position is explicit even though the app is not "directed to children". Apple's 5.1.1 (VERIFIED, developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines) says apps "may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality"; the draft therefore does not add a birth-date gate. An age gate would create knowledge and obligations under COPPA that the app cannot currently act on.

5. §3 What Allkept is and is not

Basis. Factual description carried from docs/terms.html §2, checked against docs/privacy.html §§3–9 and the store-submission data inventory. The "automated guesses" language is a disclaimer of accuracy for AI output; it is repeated in §16 so that the conspicuous disclaimer covers it.

Drafting choice. "Not a backup service" is stated twice (§3.4 and §14) because loss of saved content is the most likely consumer complaint and the limitation of liability in §17 excludes "loss of data or saved content". "Sorting is not a decision about you" is kept from the India terms because it is also the CCPA/state-privacy-law answer to automated decision-making questions.

6. §4 Your account

Basis. Facts from docs/store-submission.html §1 (Google or Apple sign-in, name only at onboarding, photo optional, no phone number as of 11 September 2026).

Drafting choice. "Everything done through your account is treated as done by you" is qualified by "unless it happened because of a security failure on our side" — an unqualified version is the kind of clause that fails TCCWNA's "clearly established right" test and looks unconscionable in California. 7. §5 How saves reach Allkept

Basis. Instagram, links, YouTube and import text carried from docs/terms.html §§2 and 5 and docs/privacy.html §§4–5. The user-agent string AllkeptBot is in supabase/functions/_shared/enrich.ts line 51 and pipeline.ts line 45. Reddit (§5.4) is new and comes from the code: enrich.ts line 121 fetches https://www.reddit.com/oembed?url=… (Reddit's public embed endpoint) and stores only the title and poster; normalize.ts lines 302–333 parse the link; apps/mobile/lib/resolve-link.ts resolves Reddit /s/ share links on the phone because Reddit refuses datacentre requests. No file in the repository calls oauth.reddit.com or any Reddit Data API endpoint, and no Reddit credentials exist.

Why say this in the terms. The legal plan (§2) asked whether Allkept uses Reddit's Data API. It does not. Stating the actual behaviour in the terms (a) tells the user truthfully what happens and (b) documents that Reddit's Data API Terms — which govern API access, registration and commercial use — are not engaged by an oEmbed preview and a stored link. Reddit's Data API Terms and Developer Terms themselves are UNVERIFIED: redditinc.com, support.reddithelp.com and archive.org were all blocked from this environment. Counsel should read them once (see counsel list).

9. §7 Your content and the licence

Basis. A hosting service needs a licence to copy, reformat (thumbnail generation), transmit and display user material; without one, each copy is technically an act reserved to the copyright owner. The licence is deliberately narrow: "solely in order to provide the Service to you, to keep it secure, and to support and troubleshoot it for you". It names the AI classification and search-index processing expressly, because the Privacy Policy discloses them and a licence that did not cover them would be incomplete.

Drafting choices. (1) "We do not use your content or your saves to train AI models" is contractual, not just a privacy statement. OpenAI's API data documentation confirms the provider side: "data sent to the OpenAI API is not used to train or improve OpenAI models (unless you explicitly opt in…)", abuse-monitoring logs are "retained for up to 30 days", and embeddings have "None" for application-state retention (VERIFIED, developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data). OpenAI's Services Agreement, Data Processing Addendum and Usage Policies returned HTTP 403 from openai.com and archive.org was blocked, so the contractual ownership/processor terms are UNVERIFIED today; the privacy policy's existing description of OpenAI as a processor is unchanged. (2) The licence "ends when you delete the item or your account", with the backup exception the privacy policy already discloses (Supabase backups up to 7 days) and the feedback exception from §13. (3) No "moral rights waiver", no "sublicensable", no "perpetual" — none is needed for a private library and each would be attacked as overreach.

Alternative considered. A broader licence "to operate and improve the Service" is standard, but "improve" is read by many users and some regulators as "train models on"; the draft says what the improvement is (security, support, troubleshooting) instead.

10. §8 Our content; app licence

Basis. Apple's Minimum Terms item "Scope of License" requires the licence to be "non-transferable" and limited to Apple devices the user owns or controls (VERIFIED, minterms link above); Apple's own Standard EULA §a grants "a nontransferable license to use the Licensed Application on any Apple-branded products that you own or control and as permitted by the Usage Rules" (VERIFIED, apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula). Google's DDA §5 has the developer grant the user "a nonexclusive, worldwide, and perpetual license to perform, display, and use the Product" and allows a separate EULA that yields to the DDA on conflict (VERIFIED, effective September 15, 2025, play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html).

Drafting choice. The reverse-engineering restriction carries the "except to the extent a law that applies to you gives you a right we cannot exclude" qualifier from the India terms; interoperability and security-research exceptions exist in several legal systems. The open-source sentence is true (the app is built on Expo/React Native, whose components carry their own licences) and prevents the "all rights reserved" statement from contradicting those licences.

11. §9 Acceptable use

Basis. Carried from docs/terms.html §7, with three additions: "cause us to break [a platform's rules]" (Meta §7 and YouTube §24 both let the platform cut the app off for a user's behaviour), "upload someone else's data export" (privacy and Meta Platform Data concerns), and the no-account-sharing/no-subscription-sharing rule (needed once there is something to share). 12. §10 Copyright and the DMCA

VERIFIED 17 U.S.C. §512, law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512:

  • §512(c)(2): the provider must designate an agent "by making available through its service, including on its website in a location accessible to the public, and by providing to the Copyright Office, substantially the following information: (A) the name, address, phone number, and electronic mail address of the agent". → §10.1 of the draft. The telephone number is marked [COUNSEL] because Pranav has kept it out of the public repository (store-submission §1); it cannot be omitted from the published terms.
  • §512(c)(3)(A)(i)–(vi): the six notice elements. → §10.2 items 1–6 track the statute one for one, with the plain-English addition "the link to the original post is usually enough" for element (iii).
  • §512(f): knowing material misrepresentation liability "for any damages, including costs and attorneys' fees". → §10.2 last paragraph.
  • §512(g)(2)(A)–(C): notify the subscriber promptly; forward the counter-notice; replace the material "not less than 10, nor more than 14, business days following receipt" unless the agent first receives notice of a court action. → §10.3 and §10.4.
  • §512(g)(3)(A)–(D): counter-notice contents, including consent to federal district court jurisdiction and acceptance of service. → §10.4 items 1–4. The "if you are outside the United States, any judicial district in which Allkept may be found" wording is the statutory formula; Allkept has no US presence, which makes this odd in practice — counsel may prefer to name a district.
  • §512(i)(1)(A): safe harbour requires a policy, of which subscribers are informed, "that provides for the termination in appropriate circumstances of subscribers and account holders … who are repeat infringers". → §10.5. The three-notices-in-twelve-months threshold is a proposal; the statute requires only that the policy be adopted, "reasonably implemented" and communicated. A stated number is easier to apply consistently but must then actually be applied — marked [COUNSEL].

VERIFIED 37 CFR §201.38, law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/201.38: designation must include the provider's full legal name and street address, alternate names the public would use to search, the agent's name, and "the physical mail address (street address or post office box), telephone number, and email address of the agent"; "a service provider's designation will expire and become invalid three years after it is registered". Fee: 37 CFR §201.3 item (25), "Designation of agent under 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(2) … or amendment or resubmission of designation — $6" (VERIFIED, law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/201.3). The Copyright Office's directory page confirms paper designations are no longer accepted and that the provider must both post the information on its site and file it (VERIFIED, copyright.gov/dmca-directory). Register "Allkept" and "All Kept" as alternate names.

Drafting choices. (1) The draft is honest that a private library "consists mainly of links back to the original" and that removal often means removing a stored preview and thumbnail — this matches Privacy Policy §20 and avoids promising to "remove content" that Allkept does not host. (2) §10.3 commits to "promptly" rather than a number of days; the legal plan's open question 7 (service level) is answered conservatively — promising "two business days" in a contract creates a breach every time a notice arrives on a holiday. (3) §10.6 routes non-copyright complaints elsewhere so that trademark or privacy complaints are not processed as DMCA notices.

Operational checklist (from the legal plan §3, unchanged): create the dmca@allkept.app mailbox; register at the Copyright Office directory ($6, three-year renewal); publish §10.1 word-for-word identical to the registration; keep a log of notices, counter-notices, dates and outcomes per account (the repeat-infringer count depends on it).

21. §19 Dispute resolution and arbitration

Statutory foundation

VERIFIED 9 U.S.C. §2, law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/2: a written arbitration provision in "a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce … shall be valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, save upon such grounds as exist at law or in equity for the revocation of any contract". The draft invokes the FAA expressly (§19.4) so that the FAA, not state arbitration law, governs — which matters for preemption.

Supreme Court cases relied on

  • VERIFIED AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion, 563 U.S. 333 (Apr. 27, 2011), law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/09-893: California's Discover Bank rule treating most class waivers in consumer contracts as unconscionable is "pre-empted by the FAA". The consumer-friendly features of AT&T's clause that the Court noted (company pays costs, arbitration where the customer lives, minimum recovery) are known from the opinion but were not in the fetched syllabus — SECONDARY; the draft copies the approach (§19.5–19.6) rather than the numbers.
  • VERIFIED American Express Co. v. Italian Colors Restaurant, 570 U.S. 228 (June 20, 2013), …/12-133: "The FAA does not permit courts to invalidate a contractual waiver of class arbitration on the ground that the plaintiff's cost of individually arbitrating a federal statutory claim exceeds the potential recovery." Even so, the draft keeps consumer costs near zero (§19.6) — cheaper than winning the argument.
  • VERIFIED Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis (May 21, 2018), …/16-285: agreements for individualised proceedings must be enforced; the saving clause protects only "generally applicable contract defenses, such as fraud, duress, or unconscionability", not defences that target arbitration.
  • VERIFIED Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana (June 15, 2022), …/20-1573: the FAA preempts California's rule only insofar as it bars dividing a representative (PAGA) action into individual and non-individual claims; wholesale waivers of non-waivable representative claims are not saved. → §19.7 and §19.9 carve out non-waivable representative and public-injunction claims instead of purporting to waive them.
  • VERIFIED Morgan v. Sundance, Inc., 596 U.S. 411 (May 23, 2022), …/21-328: "a court may not devise novel rules to favor arbitration over litigation"; the FAA's policy is to put arbitration agreements "upon the same footing as other contracts". Practical lesson: the clause must be fair on ordinary contract principles; it will get no thumb on the scale.
  • VERIFIED Coinbase, Inc. v. Suski (May 23, 2024), …/23-3: where two contracts point arbitrability in different directions, "a court must decide which contract governs". → §19.11 last sentence and §19.12 say which version of the arbitration agreement applies when terms change, and the India terms must not be shown to US users.
  • SECONDARY Jules v. Andre Balazs Properties (U.S. May 2026, per Consumer Finance Monitor, consumerfinancemonitor.com …): a federal court that stays a case under FAA §3 keeps jurisdiction to confirm or vacate the award. No drafting consequence; noted as the most recent Supreme Court arbitration decision found.

Mass arbitration and the Ninth Circuit

VERIFIED Heckman v. Live Nation Entertainment, Inc., No. 23-55770 (9th Cir. Oct. 28, 2024), cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/…/23-55770.pdf. The panel held the delegation clause and the whole arbitration agreement unconscionable under California law. Procedural unconscionability was "far above and beyond a run-of-the-mill contract-of-adhesion case" (the Terms could be changed retroactively and users were bound to New Era ADR's rules by reference). Four features of New Era's rules supported substantive unconscionability: "(1) the mass arbitration protocol, including the application of precedent from bellwether decisions to other claimants; (2) procedural limitations, such as the lack of a right to discovery; (3) a limited right of appeal; and (4) the arbitrator selection provisions." Application of California unconscionability law "was not preempted by the Federal Arbitration Act because this application relied on generally applicable principles". "As an alternate and independent ground", the FAA "does not preempt California's prohibition, under Discover Bank …, of class action waivers contained in contracts of adhesion in large-scale small-stakes consumer cases" where the arbitration is of that non-traditional kind; Judge VanDyke concurred that the FAA "does not apply to the type of mass arbitration contemplated by Live Nation's agreements". SECONDARY The Supreme Court denied certiorari in October 2025 (Daily Journal via search; Live Nation's petition of May 5, 2025 is described by Jackson Lewis, jacksonlewis.com …), so Heckman is the law of the Ninth Circuit — which includes California, the recommended governing law.

SECONDARY Jones v. Starz Entertainment, LLC, 120 F.4th 670 (9th Cir. Feb. 28, 2025), as summarised by DLA Piper, dlapiper.com …: JAMS consolidated about 7,300 individual demands before one arbitrator; the Ninth Circuit held consolidation is a procedural matter for the administrator, the claimant was not "aggrieved" under the FAA because Starz "never failed, neglected, or refused to arbitrate", and consolidation where each claimant still pursues an individual claim is unlike the bellwether preclusion condemned in Heckman. SECONDARY O'Dell v. Aya Healthcare (9th Cir. Apr. 1, 2026, per Duane Morris, blogs.duanemorris.com …): a district court may not use non-mutual offensive collateral estoppel from selected arbitral awards to invalidate hundreds of other individual arbitration agreements — consent to arbitrate is individual.

How §19.10 was drafted in response. The coordinated-filings clause (a) adopts the AAA's own Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules rather than inventing a bespoke protocol (the New Era problem); (b) states that "no decision in one case binds any other claimant, or binds us as to any other claimant" — the opposite of bellwether preclusion; (c) leaves discovery to the AAA rules (§19.5) and gives nobody an appeal beyond the FAA's grounds; (d) tolls limitation periods for waiting claimants; (e) makes mediation optional and pays for it; (f) gives the enforceability question to a court, so that if a court dislikes it, it can sever §19.10 alone (§19.13) rather than condemn the whole agreement — Heckman refused to sever because too many terms were tainted at once. AAA definitions (VERIFIED, Mass Arbitration Supplementary Rules amended and effective April 1, 2024, copy at acerislaw.com/…/Mass-Arbitration-Supplementary-Rules.pdf; AAA consumer mass arbitration page, adr.org/industries/consumer-mass-arbitration): "25 or more similar demands for arbitration" with "consistent or coordinated representation across cases"; a Process Arbitrator; counsel affirmations; the rules "apply when the arbitration agreement provides for AAA rules". AAA's January 15, 2024 announcement (VERIFIED, adr.org/press-releases/…) describes the flat initiation fee, staged fees and virtual hearings.

Consumer fairness standards

VERIFIED AAA consumer arbitration page, adr.org/consumer: the Consumer Due Process Protocol requires a "reasonably convenient location", "reasonable cost to consumer", "small claims court instead of arbitration", an "independent and impartial arbitrator" and the "right to representation"; "the most the consumer would be asked to pay for a AAA consumer arbitration case is $225"; the business pays the rest and all arbitrator compensation; a business must register its clause in the AAA Consumer Clause Registry before the AAA will administer it. VERIFIED JAMS Consumer Minimum Standards (effective May 1, 2024), jamsadr.com/consumer-minimum-standards: reciprocal obligation; clear notice; all statutory remedies preserved; neutral arbitrator; convenient location; no discouraging counsel; consumer fee capped at $250; no fee-shifting against a losing consumer beyond what the law allows; discovery of relevant non-privileged information; written award. Every one of these is reflected in §19.4–19.7 even though the AAA is the named provider, so the clause would also be administrable by JAMS if the AAA is unavailable.

California fee-payment rules

VERIFIED Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §1281.97 (leginfo … CCP 1281.97) and §1281.98 (… 1281.98): if the drafting party does not pay required fees within 30 days of the due date it "is in material breach of the arbitration agreement, is in default of the arbitration, and waives its right to compel arbitration", and the consumer may withdraw to court (with fee-shifting and sanctions) or compel arbitration with fees paid by the drafter. → §19.6 "Paying on time". Operationally: whoever handles the AAA invoices must pay them within days, not weeks. Later amendments to these sections (2024–25) are UNVERIFIED.

Public injunctive relief

SECONDARY McGill v. Citibank, N.A. (Cal. Apr. 7, 2017), per Akin Gump and others (search results; the Justia opinion page was not fetched): a pre-dispute waiver of the right to seek public injunctive relief under the CLRA, UCL and FAL is unenforceable under California law and the FAA does not preempt that rule. → §19.7 second paragraph and the §19.9 carve-out (individual claims arbitrated first; the public-injunction request decided by a court afterwards). Some clauses instead route public injunctive relief to the arbitrator; either satisfies McGill, and a court is the safer forum choice for a foreign defendant because it avoids an argument that the arbitrator cannot grant that relief.

Opt-out (§19.11)

A 30-day opt-out is the single feature most often cited by courts (including in Concepcion-era Ninth Circuit cases) as reducing procedural unconscionability, because the term is no longer imposed on a take-it-or-leave-it basis. The draft makes the opt-out (a) by email to an address that exists today, (b) available to existing users from the date of notice, and (c) explicitly "we will not treat you differently". DECISION Product must log opt-outs against the account so that a later motion to compel is never filed against someone who opted out.

Changes (§19.12)

Douglas and Heckman (retroactive unilateral modification) drive the 30-day notice, the fresh opt-out, and the rule that changes never apply to disputes already noticed or filed.

Enforcement in India

SECONDARY Search results (Nishith Desai Associates paper "Enforcing U.S. Arbitral Awards and Judgments in India", nishithdesai.com …, not fetched) state that the United States is a territory notified by the Government of India under section 44 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, so a US arbitral award is enforceable in India under the New York Convention, whereas a US court judgment is not directly executable there. This is a reason arbitration serves both sides: the consumer gets a forum whose award reaches the company's assets; the company gets a single individual forum. UNVERIFIED against the gazette notification itself.

What was deliberately left out

  • No jury waiver (see TCCWNA note above).
  • No confidentiality obligation on the consumer (JAMS standard; also a Heckman-style procedural complaint).
  • No shortened limitation period.
  • No "loser pays" or fee-shifting against the consumer.
  • No delegation of the class-waiver, mass-filing and small-claims questions to the arbitrator (§19.8) — courts are more comfortable enforcing a clause that lets them decide those.
  • No bespoke arbitration provider: AAA, with a court-appointment fallback under the FAA rather than a company-chosen substitute.

22. §20 Governing law and venue — the recommendation

Recommendation: the FAA for §19; California law for everything else, with a savings clause for the user's home-state consumer laws; San Francisco County courts for the residual cases; small claims where the user lives. Marked [COUNSEL] in the draft.

Reasoning.

  1. A US governing law is needed. Keeping Indian law for US consumers (the current terms) invites an unconscionability finding on the whole document, is unlikely to be honoured by a US court against a consumer, and would leave the arbitration clause standing on Indian law it does not need.
  2. The chosen state must have a reasonable relationship to the deal. Allkept has no US office, so the connection must come from the transaction. California is where the service is delivered from the user's point of view: both storefronts (Apple, Google), the AI processor (OpenAI), the web host (Vercel) and the backend provider (Supabase Inc.) are California companies, and Apple's Standard EULA (§i, "governed by the laws of the State of California") and Google's DDA (§16, California law, Santa Clara courts) already put the app ecosystem under California law (VERIFIED, stdeula and DDA links above). New York or Delaware would have no comparable connection.
  3. Predictability. The rules this document must satisfy — §1668, §1670.5, §1751, §1789.3, §1798.192, the ARL, McGill, CCP 1281.97–98, Heckman, Chabolla — are California rules. Drafting to them and then choosing California law means the document is tested against the standard it was built for; choosing another state adds a second set of unknowns without removing California's, because California statutes apply to California residents regardless.
  4. Cost. California is the most consumer-protective forum, so the choice concedes something for users elsewhere. The savings clause limits that: non-California users keep their own non-waivable protections and gain California's contract rules.

Alternatives considered. (a) The law of the user's state of residence. Maximises enforceability of the arbitration clause (a New York user's clause is tested under New York's friendlier unconscionability law) and cannot be attacked for lack of a relationship; the cost is fifty possible bodies of contract law and no single yardstick. A genuine option; counsel may prefer it. (b) New York. Predictable commercial law and home of the AAA-ICDR, but no transactional connection and no reason a consumer would expect it. (c) Delaware. Common for US-incorporated startups; MECA is not one, and there is no connection at all. (d) India. Rejected for US users for the reasons in item 1; it remains right for the India terms.

Venue. San Francisco County is the seat of the providers named above and a court experienced with technology contracts and foreign parties. The clause does not stop a small-claims filing in the user's county (required by the AAA protocol and most state small-claims rules) and does not purport to strip a user of a non-waivable home-forum right.

28. §26–27 General terms and contact

Standard boilerplate with three deliberate features: (1) assignment is allowed only to a successor that "must honour these terms and the Privacy Policy", matching Privacy Policy §12's promise to tell users before data moves; (2) force majeure excludes payment obligations and the right to cancel, so it cannot be read as suspending consumer rights; (3) the "short version" and headings are declared non-operative so the summary cannot be used to vary the clauses.
