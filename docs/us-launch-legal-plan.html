3. DMCA safe harbour

Allkept stores copies of other people's pictures (thumbnail snapshots, up to 4 MB) and captions on its servers, in each user's private library. For a personal bookmarking tool that is a defensible use, but the reliable protection in the US against copyright claims for user-stored material is the safe harbour in 17 U.S.C. §512 — and you only get it if you:

  • Register a designated agent in the US Copyright Office's DMCA Designated Agent Directory (online account, small fee — about US$6 per designation from memory; designations expire after three years). The agent can be Pranav at the company address, with an email such as dmca@allkept.app.
  • Publish the agent's contact on the site (a short "Copyright" section on the terms page or a /copyright page) — the registration and the published contact must match.
  • Have a takedown process: on a valid notice, remove or disable the item promptly, tell the user, accept counter-notices, restore after the statutory wait if no lawsuit follows. It can be a one-page checklist and an inbox.
  • Adopt and state a repeat-infringer policy in the terms (accounts that repeatedly infringe are terminated) and be able to act on it.

What this does not require: scanning content, or removing anything without a notice.

4. Privacy: US state laws, BIPA, TCPA, COPPA

State privacy laws

There is no federal comprehensive privacy law. California's CCPA/CPRA and the roughly twenty similar state laws mostly apply above thresholds — for California, from memory: about US$25M annual revenue, or personal data of 100,000+ California residents/households, or half of revenue from selling or sharing data. A launch-stage app is below all three. Two things are still worth doing now because they cost nothing and reviewers read the page: a short "Your rights in the United States" section (access, deletion, correction — all of which the app already provides; and a plain "we do not sell or share personal information for advertising"), and keeping the data inventory accurate as the product changes (phone and gender out, photo optional).

Biometrics (Illinois BIPA and cousins)

A stored profile photo is not a biometric. Face geometry extracted from it is, and Illinois BIPA carries statutory damages per violation with a private right of action — it is the single most litigated privacy statute in the US. Rule for the product: never run face detection, recognition or "smart crop by face" on profile photos or thumbnails without a lawyer's sign-off and written consent flows. Texas and Washington have similar laws.

Phone numbers (TCPA)

Once the phone field goes, this is moot for new users. For any numbers already stored: never send SMS or automated calls without prior express consent; simplest is to not use them at all, and to delete the column when the app no longer reads it (a later, separate migration).

Children (COPPA)

18+ in the terms and both store age ratings cover it. Do not add features that would appeal to children or ask for a birth date you then have to act on.

Data location

The backend is in Mumbai. The US has no data-localisation requirement for this kind of service; the policy already discloses the location. Nothing to do.

5. Terms of service for US users

The current terms are governed by Indian law with Bengaluru courts, and were drafted without a lawyer. A US consumer can sue at home regardless of what the terms say. Before Allkept takes money from US users, a lawyer who does US consumer-tech work should make these calls:

  • Arbitration clause with a class-action waiver — standard in US consumer apps; it is the clause with the most practical value.
  • Limitation of liability and warranty disclaimers that survive US state consumer-protection rules (some states limit what can be disclaimed).
  • Governing law and venue — keep India, or choose a US state; either is workable, the lawyer will have a view.
  • Subscription terms — see §6.
  • DMCA section — agent contact, notice requirements, repeat-infringer policy (§3).
  • Acceptable use and platform pass-through — users must have the right to save what they save; the platforms' own terms flow down.

Ask for a fixed-fee review of the existing document rather than a rewrite; the factual sections already describe the real service.

6. The US paid tier — what it changes

Decision: a subscription for the US storefront only; India free; no ads anywhere. Consequences to build in from the start:

  • Stores as merchant of record. Digital features must be sold through Apple's in-app purchase and Google Play Billing (Apple guideline 3.1.1). The stores collect US sales tax, handle refunds and payouts. No Stripe, no web checkout for app features.
  • Storefront gating, not IP gating. The subscription is made available only in the US in both consoles; the app decides whether to show a paywall from the storefront the store reports (StoreKit's storefront on iOS, the billing country on Android). An Indian account sees the full free product.
  • Auto-renewal disclosures. Price, billing period, that it renews until cancelled, and how to cancel — shown before purchase in the app and in the listing. Apple and Google enforce this; California's automatic-renewal law and similar state laws sit behind it, and the stores' flows satisfy them when used as designed.
  • Restore purchases on iOS, a visible cancel path, and no features locked behind the paywall that the free tier promised earlier — changing what free users get after launch draws complaints and reviews.
  • Two products in one binary. The listing copy must be true in both countries: describe the paid features as "available in the United States".

7. Money, tax and the Indian entity

MECA Engineering Solutions (OPC) Private Limited can sell to US users through the stores without a US entity. Things to put in front of the CA (not legal advice, and outside what I can verify):

  • Apple: complete the tax forms in App Store Connect (W-8BEN-E for a non-US company; the India–US treaty applies). Google Play: the payments profile asks for the equivalent.
  • India side: store payouts are export of services; GST treatment (zero-rated with a Letter of Undertaking, from memory) and FEMA reporting are the CA's call.
  • US side: with Apple and Google as merchant of record, US sales tax is theirs. Avoid direct web billing until there is a reason to take on state-by-state nexus.

8. Trademark

Search the USPTO's trademark database for "Allkept" and "All Kept" in the software (class 9) and online-services (class 42) classes, and the Indian registry likewise. Rebranding after launch is expensive; searching is free. Filing is optional at this stage — roughly US$350 per class in the US from memory — and worth it once the name has traction. Store names are first-come, and the App Store record already holds "Allkept".

9. Security incidents and breach notification

All fifty US states have breach-notification laws with deadlines that start when you learn of an incident. What to have on paper before launch (one page): who is on call, how a suspected incident is confirmed (Supabase logs, auth logs), who decides on notification, and the template for telling affected users. Data minimisation is the best defence — dropping the phone number and making the photo optional shrinks what an incident could expose.

10. Ongoing obligations

  • Quarterly: users by US state and revenue, against the state-law thresholds (§4). Crossing one adds real obligations (privacy-rights request handling within set deadlines, "Do Not Sell" links where applicable).
  • Every three years: renew the DMCA designation before it lapses.
  • On every platform-terms change notice from Meta, Google or Reddit: re-read against the code paths in §2.
  • On every new data field or SDK: update the privacy policy, the store labels and the data-safety form in the same change.
