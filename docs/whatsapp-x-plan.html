1. The short version

WhatsApp is the cheaper and better of the two, and it is the one nobody expects. A "forward it to Allkept's WhatsApp number" door is the same shape as the Instagram DM door that already exists — a Meta webhook, a link code, a reply in the thread — and Meta charges nothing at all for it in Allkept's flow: the person messages first, which opens a 24-hour window, and inside that window both their message and Allkept's reply are free. Unlike Instagram, it needs no App Review: Meta's own documentation says a direct developer accessing only their own business data does not need App Review or Advanced access. What it needs is a spare phone number that has never been on WhatsApp, a few days of work, and a database migration. The one real hazard is a clause in the WhatsApp Business Solution Terms about AI assistants — §7 explains it, and it deserves a careful read before a line of code is written.

X is the opposite: mostly built, cheap to polish, and expensive the moment you want anything real from it. Links are already detected, canonicalised, deduplicated and enriched; the logo is in the app. But X's oEmbed returns no picture and no title — I confirmed this against the live endpoint — so every X save today is a text-only card, and each one quietly pays for a second page fetch that returns nothing. Fixing the card is a day. Playing an X video in place is two or three days. And the thing people actually want — "bring in my X bookmarks" — now costs real money: X killed the free tier in February 2026, moved to pay-per-use, and prices a Post read at $0.005. A person with 500 bookmarks costs $2.50 to import, once. A hundred such people cost $250. Worse, likes are no longer purchasable at all on any self-serve plan.

What is not possible: reading a person's WhatsApp chats, starred messages or saved messages — WhatsApp is end-to-end encrypted and publishes no such API, and the Cloud API only ever sees messages sent to the business's own number. And on X, reading a person's likes: "Following, Likes, and Quote-Posts via the API have been removed from all self-serve tiers" (X changelog, 16 April 2026).

In plain English. WhatsApp is a free door that costs you a phone number and about a week. X is already half-built but currently makes ugly, pictureless cards — a day fixes that. Importing someone's X bookmarks is possible but you pay X about two and a half dollars per person who does it, and importing their likes is simply not for sale any more.

3. WhatsApp — every door

(a) Share sheet → Allkept  VERIFY

Already built, in the sense that Allkept accepts a shared link from any app. Nothing WhatsApp-specific exists and nothing needs to. The honest state is "believed to work, never tried". Two things to check on a real phone:

  • iOS: long-pressing a text message in WhatsApp offers Forward, Copy, Star, Delete — I could not confirm from documentation that it offers the system share sheet. Media messages do have a share button. If text messages do not, the practical iOS path is: tap the link (it opens Safari or the destination app) and share from there, or copy the link and paste it into Allkept.
  • Android: long-press a message → overflow → Share hands ACTION_SEND text/plain, which ShareActivity is registered for. The shared text may be the whole quoted message rather than the bare URL; saveLink() already extracts the first URL from surrounding text, so this should be fine.

(b) "Send it to Allkept's WhatsApp number"  GAP — possible, free, and no App Review

This is the door worth having. The shape is identical to the Instagram DM door already in the repo, and the pipeline underneath is entirely reusable.

New business portfolios are initially capped at two registered business phone numbers, rising to 20 on business verification or a 2,000 messaging limit.

WebhookThe contents of any message sent from a WhatsApp user to your business phone number is communicated via webhook. The payload is object: "whatsapp_business_account" → entry[].changes[].value.messages[], each with from (the sender's phone number), id, timestamp, type, and for a text message text.body. The id is the natural idempotency key, exactly as the Instagram message id is today.
ThroughputBusiness phone numbers can send up to 80 messages per second by default, and 1 message every 6 seconds to the same WhatsApp user. Far above anything Allkept would do.

The 24-hour window, and why it makes this free

A message from a person to the business opens a 24 hour customer service window. Inside it, the business may send ordinary (non-template) messages, and All non-template messages are free. Allkept's whole interaction lives inside that window: the person sends a link, Allkept replies within seconds. Meta's own words on the two halves:

  • Messages sent from a WhatsApp user to a business are not charged.
  • Effective November 1, 2024 – Service conversations are now free for all businesses.
  • And from the business-facing pricing page: We do not charge for service messages.

Per-message charging (Effective July 1, 2025, Meta charges on a per-message basis) applies to template messages — marketing, utility, authentication — sent outside an open window. Allkept would send none in the normal flow. The messaging limits Meta publishes (250 unique users, rising to 2,000 and beyond) are explicitly the maximum number of unique WhatsApp user phone numbers your business can deliver messages to, outside of a customer service window, within a moving 24-hour period — so they do not bind a reply-only bot either.

What would have to be built

  1. supabase/functions/whatsapp-webhook: the GET verification handshake, X-Hub-Signature-256 validation against the app secret (the Instagram webhook already does exactly this), the messages[] loop, dedupe on message id.
  2. Pairing a WhatsApp number to an Allkept account. The Instagram door's link-code mechanism (link_codes, consumeLinkCode) transfers directly: the app shows a code, the person sends it to the number, the webhook claims it.
  3. One migration widening the four check constraints listed in §2.
  4. Reply text and the reply-window guard — the replies table and the not_after column already model "do not send outside the window"; only the window length changes (24 hours rather than Instagram's).
  5. A settings screen entry and copy, mirroring "Connect Instagram".

(c) WhatsApp chat export as an import source  GAP — possible, with a privacy question attached

WhatsApp's "Export chat" produces, per chat, a zip containing a plain-text transcript (_chat.txt on iOS; WhatsApp Chat with <name>.txt on Android) with one line per message — date, time, sender, text — and a placeholder where media was, if "Without Media" is chosen. A link in that file is simply a URL sitting in the message body, which extractFirstUrl already knows how to find.

What I could not verify. The official WhatsApp Help Center article on exporting chats renders its body in JavaScript; neither the documentation fetcher nor a plain request could read a single word of it (the page returned 1.2 MB of script and no prose). The commonly repeated limits — about 10,000 messages with media and 40,000 without — come only from third-party blogs. [UNVERIFIED] Treat them as a hint, not a spec, and confirm on a real phone before designing around them.

Two things make this different from the Instagram import, and both are decisions rather than engineering:

  • It is per-chat, not per-account. There is no "export everything". A person would have to export each conversation they care about. That is a real friction, and it caps the feature's usefulness to "that one group chat where everyone posts restaurants".
  • It contains other people's messages. An Instagram saved-posts export is entirely the person's own activity. A WhatsApp transcript is a conversation — names, timestamps, and the full text of what other people wrote. Even if Allkept only keeps the URLs, the file it reads holds far more. That needs a deliberate answer in the privacy policy and in the import screen's copy, and it is the kind of thing an App Store reviewer will notice.

(d) What is not possible on WhatsApp

DoorPossible?Why
Read a person's starred / saved messagesNOT POSSIBLEWhatsApp publishes no API for a person's own messages, starred or otherwise. Personal chats are end-to-end encrypted and the star lives on the device.
"Connect WhatsApp" and read historyNOT POSSIBLEThe Cloud API is a business-messaging product. A business sees only messages sent to its own number, from the moment the webhook is live. There is no backfill and no access to the person's other conversations.
Read links from a group Allkept is not inNOT POSSIBLESame reason. (Adding a business number to a group is not a supported Cloud API pattern either.)
A WhatsApp "platform" for saved linksNOT POSSIBLEWhatsApp hosts nothing publicly. A wa.me link is a deep link, not a post. Filing it as web is correct.
In plain English. You can give Allkept its own WhatsApp number that people forward links to, it costs nothing per message, and unlike Instagram you do not have to wait three weeks for Meta to approve it. What you cannot do is reach into someone's WhatsApp and pull out the links they already have — the only ones you ever see are the ones they deliberately send you. The chat-export route is the exception, and it drags other people's private messages along with it, so it is a decision about principles as much as about code.

4. X — every door

(a) Share sheet and paste  DONE VERIFY

Both already work through the generic path. Pasting an x.com or twitter.com link into SaveLinkField produces a correct canonical URL and external id — there are unit tests for exactly these shapes. Sharing from the X app has never been tried. The only X-specific wrinkle is t.co, which is flagged for expansion and resolved by the redirect-following fetcher.

(b) oEmbed via publish.x.com  DONE — free, public, no token

Checked live on 12 September 2026, from this machine, using Allkept's own AllkeptBot user agent:

  • https://publish.twitter.com/oembed → 301 Moved Permanently → https://publish.x.com/oembed → 200. Allkept follows it, so nothing is broken; the address in enrich.ts is simply a year out of date.
  • X's own documentation for the endpoint states plainly: "Requires authentication? No" and "Rate limited: No".
  • The JSON carries: url, author_name, author_url, html, width, height (null for a post), type, cache_age (3153600000 — a hundred years), provider_name, provider_url, version. No title. No thumbnail_url.
  • It accepts the canonical x.com/<handle>/status/<id> form Allkept stores, and the legacy twitter.com form.
  • A profile URL returns a timeline snippet with an empty title and no author.
  • A non-existent status returns 404 with an HTML error body.

Server-side fetches will run from Singapore (ap-southeast-1) once the region fix lands. I have no reason to expect a different answer from there — the endpoint is a public CDN-served JSON API — but it is worth one check after the region change, because the one thing I did see this machine refused on was X's other hosts (developer.x.com answering 402, help.x.com answering 403).

(c) The X API for bookmarks and likes  GAP / NOT POSSIBLE

The pricing model changed completely in 2026

X's own changelog and pricing pages, read 12 September 2026:

  • 6 February 2026 — X launched pay-per-use pricing. Today's introduction page says: The X API uses pay-per-usage pricing. No subscriptions—pay only for what you use. and Purchase credits upfront. Deducted as you use the API. The changelog entry notes Public Utility Apps continue to receive free scaled access — a category Allkept would not qualify for.
  • 16 April 2026 — Effective April 20, 2026, we introduced new "Owned Reads" pricing at $0.001 per resource, and, critically: Following, Likes, and Quote-Posts via the API have been removed from all self-serve tiers.
  • The changelog also says Basic and Pro plans remain available, and existing subscribers can opt in to Pay-Per-Use. I could not load the page that lists their prices — developer.x.com answered 402 to every request from this machine. [UNVERIFIED]

Bookmarks — buyable

GET /2/users/{id}/bookmarks exists and is documented. Authentication is OAuth 2.0 user context with the scopes tweet.read, bookmark.read, users.read. max_results is minimum: 1, maximum: 100, with pagination_token / next_token paging. The documentation page I read did not state a rate limit or a ceiling on how far back bookmarks go; secondary sources say 800 most-recent, which I could not confirm officially. [UNVERIFIED]

The price trap. There are two read prices, and Allkept pays the higher one. "Owned Reads" is $0.001 per resource, but the definition is narrow: Owned Reads are requests made by your own developer app for your own data, and These endpoints qualify for Owned Read pricing when {id} matches the authenticated user and that user is the owner of the developer app. Allkept's users are not the owner of Allkept's developer app. So a bookmark read is billed at the ordinary Posts: Read, $0.005 per resource.

Likes — not buyable at any self-serve price

GET /2/users/{id}/liked_tweets is still documented (OAuth 2.0 with like.read, users.read, tweet.read, max_results 5–100), but the 16 April 2026 changelog removes Likes from all self-serve tiers. Reading a person's likes would therefore require Enterprise. NOT POSSIBLE for a launch-stage app.

What Allkept would have to build for bookmarks

  1. An X developer account and app, with credits bought up front. X's console lets you Set a maximum amount you can spend per billing cycle; when credits run out, API requests will be blocked until you add credits to cover the negative balance.
  2. OAuth 2.0 Authorization Code with PKCE inside the app — a real "Sign in with X" flow, a third identity provider alongside Google and Apple, token storage, and refresh (offline.access).
  3. A sync job that pages 100 at a time and normalises each status URL through the existing X handler.
  4. A deletion obligation: X's policy requires stored Content to be kept in sync and removed within 24 hours of deletion or a request (§7). That is a background job Allkept does not have today for any platform.

(d) X's data export  [UNVERIFIED]

X offers "Download an archive of your data" from account settings, delivered as a zip of JSON and HTML. I could not verify its contents on an official page: help.x.com answered HTTP 403 to every request from this machine. What third-party sources consistently say, and what the X developer forum has had open threads about for years, is that the archive contains likes but not bookmarks, and that X says preparation can take 24 hours or longer. If that holds, the export is an interesting inversion: it gives you exactly the thing the API no longer sells (likes) and not the thing the API does sell (bookmarks). That would make the export the only route to a person's X likes — and a free one.

This is the single highest-value fact in this document that I could not confirm. It is also easy to confirm: request the archive on Pranav's own X account and look inside. Until then it stays [UNVERIFIED] and Phase 3 below is written as a spike, not a build.

(e) Playing an X video inside Allkept  GAP

X has no documented iframe URL of the Instagram /embed/ kind. The official route is two parts: An HTML snippet hosted in your web page, and the X for Websites JavaScript to transform that code into a fully-rendered Post. The script is now served from https://platform.x.com/widgets.js (confirmed by reading the html field of a live oEmbed response with omit_script off).

Two ways to do it in the existing EmbedPlayer WebView:

  • Supported route. Load a tiny local HTML document containing the oEmbed html blockquote plus the widgets.js script tag. This is exactly what X asks third parties to do, it satisfies the Display Requirements automatically (§7), and it happens to fit the existing player: EmbedPlayer's height-measuring script already looks for blockquote among its selectors. It does mean embed.ts would return markup for X rather than a URL, which is a small shape change to an interface that currently returns string | null.
  • Undocumented shortcut. https://platform.twitter.com/embed/Tweet.html?id=<status id> returns 200 text/html (verified live; platform.x.com/embed/Tweet.html redirects to it). This is the iframe widgets.js itself creates, and it would drop straight into embedUrl() with no interface change. It is not in X's documentation, so it can change or disappear without notice, and building on it is the sort of thing §7's "public, documented endpoints only" rule exists to prevent. I would not use it.

Note the ceiling: many X posts are text with no media at all, so "play in place" for X really means "show the post as X renders it" — quote tweets, images, threads and video included. That is arguably more valuable than video playback alone, because it is also the fix for the missing picture (§5).

5. What an X card actually looks like today

This is worth spelling out because it is the difference between "X is supported" and "X feels supported", and because the unit test hides it.

What publish.x.com returns for a real post
------------------------------------------
  author_name   "U.S. Department of the Interior"
  author_url    "https://x.com/Interior"
  html          "<blockquote…>…</blockquote>"
  width         550        height  null
  cache_age     3153600000
  (no title)   (no thumbnail_url)

What enrich.ts does with it
---------------------------
  title                 -> never set          (there is no title field)
  author_name           -> set                OK
  author_handle         -> set from author_url
  thumbnail_url_remote  -> never set          (there is no thumbnail field)
  text                  -> stripTags(html)
  askThePage            -> TRUE, always       (because the thumbnail is missing)
    -> fetches x.com/<handle>/status/<id>
    -> 37 KB of JavaScript, no og: tags, no <title>
    -> learns nothing, logs "link-preview tags absent"
  status                -> "ready"

The text that lands in the database
-----------------------------------
  "Happy 50th anniversary to the Wilderness Act! Here's a great wilderness photo
   from @YosemiteNPS. #Wilderness50 pic.twitter.com/HMhbyTg18X&mdash; U.S.
   Department of the Interior (@Interior) September 3, 2014"
                                             ^^^^^^^^^ literal, and no space

Three separate defects, all fixable in the same change:

  1. No picture, ever. Not a bug in Allkept — X's oEmbed simply does not carry one. The fix is either to render the official embed (§4e) or to accept a text card and design it to look deliberate rather than broken.
  2. A wasted fetch on every X save. askThePage can never succeed for X, so the second request is pure latency and pure cost. Guarding it (platform !== "x", or better: only ask the page when the platform's own page is known to serve preview tags) removes it.
  3. A literal &mdash; glued to the caption. decodeEntities knows six named entities. X uses a seventh. And the author byline is arguably not "text the person saved" at all — Allkept already stores author_name separately, so stripping the trailing byline would leave the tweet, which is what belongs in the card.
Why the tests did not catch this. supabase/functions/tests/enrich.test.ts:64 mocks publish.twitter.com/oembed with { author_name: "Naval", html: "<blockquote><p>Seek wealth, not money</p></blockquote>" } — a response with no &mdash;, no byline, and no expectation about a thumbnail. The mock is kinder than reality. A fixture captured from the live endpoint would have failed.
In plain English. Right now, every X link you save turns into a grey card with the tweet's words, the author's name, and a stray "&mdash;" in the middle of the sentence. It also quietly makes a second trip to x.com that always comes back empty. About a day of work makes X cards look like they belong.

WhatsApp: the AI-provider clause. The WhatsApp Business Solution Terms (last modified 6 March 2026) prohibit providers of large language models, generative artificial intelligence platforms, general-purpose artificial intelligence assistants, or similar technologies from using the WhatsApp Business Solution for the purposes of providing, delivering, offering, selling, or otherwise making available such technologies when such technologies are the primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality being made available for use, as determined by Meta in its sole discretion. There is a carve-out for users with EEA or Brazil numbers.

My reading: Allkept is a link locker whose AI sorts things after they arrive — ancillary, not primary. The bot answers "Saved. Filed under Food", it does not answer questions. But the determination is explicitly Meta's alone, and Allkept's own marketing concepts include an "AI recall" idea (docs/design/welcome-concepts/concept-3-ai-recall.png). If the WhatsApp bot ever becomes "ask Allkept a question and it answers", this clause is squarely in the way. Decide the bot's scope before building it, and keep it a save-and-confirm bot. Relatedly, Meta has published an AI Providers pricing policy charging such providers for non-template messages from 16 February 2026 — the free-inside-the-window arithmetic in §6 assumes Allkept is not classified as one.

Also: a search result indicated WhatsApp's business terms are being updated effective 23 September 2026, eleven days from now. [UNVERIFIED] — re-read the terms on that date before building.
X: the Display Requirements apply to the card Allkept already ships. X's Display Requirements state that when showing a Post you must show the author's profile picture, @username and display name (each linking to the profile), the timestamp linking to the permalink, and the Post action icons or a "View on X" link; that The post text must be displayed on a line below the author's display name and @username, and may not be altered or modified; and that the X logo must always be reasonably visible. They apply to anyone displaying Posts, not only API users. The Developer Policy adds: If you don't use X for Websites to display content, then you must use the X API to retrieve the most current version available for display.

Allkept's X card today shows the logo and the author name, and shows the tweet text altered — stripped of its links, with a literal &mdash; in it. On a strict reading that is a Post displayed outside X for Websites without the API, with modified text. Nobody is going to sue over a personal bookmarking app, and Allkept is not a public site — but this is exactly the class of thing the US-launch platform-terms audit exists to find, and it has a clean fix: render the official embed (§4e), which X says handles the requirements automatically, and keep the plain text only as the search index, never as the display.
X: the 24-hour deletion obligation. If you store X Content offline, you must keep it up to date with the current state of that content on X, and you must delete or modify any content you have if it is deleted or modified on X. This must be done as soon as reasonably possible, or within 24 hours after receiving a request to do so by X or the applicable X account owner. The Policy also allows distributing up to 1,500,000 Post IDs to any entity in 30 days, and displaying up to 500 public Post objects per person per day by non-automated means — neither of which Allkept would come near. The deletion duty is the one that bites, and Allkept has no such job for any platform today. It becomes a genuine obligation the moment a bookmarks import stores hundreds of tweets per user. Do not ship a bookmarks import without a refresh-or-expire job. (This is the same shape of problem as forlater.md item 17, YouTube's 30-day storage rule — worth solving once, generically, for every platform.)

Brand and store listings

The logos come from packages/platform-assets ("official platform artwork", 9 Sep) with provenance recorded. Naming X and WhatsApp in a store listing is the same trademark question as Instagram and YouTube, already open in docs/store-submission.html §7. One extra note for WhatsApp: if Allkept ever publishes its own WhatsApp number, that number and its display name go through Meta's display-name review, and the name people see in WhatsApp is Meta's to approve, not Allkept's to choose.

In plain English. The WhatsApp door is fine as long as the bot stays a bot that says "saved" and never becomes a chatbot that answers questions — Meta has a rule about that and decides on its own what counts. On X, the real issue is that X has rules about how you are allowed to show a tweet, and Allkept's current card does not follow them. The fix is the same as the fix for the missing picture: show X's own embed. And if you ever import someone's bookmarks, you take on a duty to delete a tweet from your database within a day of it being deleted on X.

8. The plan, in phases

Phase 1 — make X cards look like they belong (about a day)

  • Point oembedUrl's x case at https://publish.x.com/oembed, removing a redirect hop from every X save.
  • Stop the pointless second fetch: askThePage must not fire for X, where it can never learn anything. (Consider generalising this — the condition that matters is "does this platform's own page serve preview tags", which is a per-platform fact, not a per-item one.)
  • Fix the text: add mdash and ndash to decodeEntities, and strip X's trailing "— Author (@handle) Date" byline, which Allkept already stores in its own columns.
  • Design decision: what an X card looks like with no picture. Today it is a grey box with a paragraph. It should look deliberate — the X mark, the author, the tweet, a quiet ground.
  • Replace the hand-written test mock with a fixture captured from the live endpoint, so the next surprise fails a test instead of shipping.
  • Name X in the welcome copy, if the answer to decision 1 is yes.

Phase 2 — the WhatsApp number door (four to six days, plus waiting on a number)

  • Before any code: obtain a phone number that has never been on WhatsApp, and re-read the WhatsApp Business Solution Terms (§7) after their 23 September update.
  • One migration widening the four check constraints in §2.
  • supabase/functions/whatsapp-webhook: verification handshake, signature check, messages[] loop, dedupe on message id, link-code pairing — all of it modelled on instagram-webhook, which already solves every one of these problems.
  • Reuse capture → runPipeline unchanged. Nothing about enrichment or classification is platform-door-specific.
  • Reply text inside the 24-hour window, using the existing replies table and its not_after guard.
  • A "Connect WhatsApp" settings entry mirroring "Connect Instagram", and the same delete-by-message and "stop replying" controls the Instagram door has.
  • Privacy policy and store data-safety entries: a new identifier (the sender's phone number) enters the system. Note that Pranav just removed phone-number collection from onboarding (forlater.md item 15) — this reintroduces phone numbers through a different door, for a different purpose, and the policy must say so plainly.

Phase 3 — the X archive spike (half a day, then decide)

  • Request "Download an archive of your data" on a real X account and look inside. Answer three questions: are likes there, are bookmarks there, and what is the file shape.
  • If likes are in the archive, that is the only route to them that exists, and it is free — a strong case for an X import that mirrors the Instagram one.
  • If bookmarks are not there (which is what everything outside X's own site says), then bookmarks are API-or-nothing, and decision 4 below becomes real.
  • Nothing gets built in this phase. It is a fact-finding exercise that costs half a day and unblocks a decision worth thousands of dollars.

Phase 4 — play X posts inside Allkept (two to three days)

  • Extend embed.ts to return X's official embed. This changes its return shape from "a URL" to "a URL or a document", which touches EmbedPlayer — a deliberate, contained interface change, not a refactor.
  • Use platform.x.com/widgets.js with the oEmbed html. Not the undocumented iframe.
  • EmbedPlayer's existing "keep the tap inside the card" and height-measurement work carries over — its selector list already includes blockquote.
  • This is also the Display Requirements fix (§7). Two problems, one change.

Phase 5 — WhatsApp chat-export import (three to four days) — only if Phase 2 lands well

  • A .txt reader beside the existing JSON reader in import-saves: same upload path, same size limits, same batching, same "wrong file" refusals.
  • Parse date, sender and body; pull every URL out of every body; dedupe; run the cheap classifier tier used for back catalogues.
  • Copy that is honest about what the file contains and what Allkept keeps from it (the links, and nothing else).

What this plan deliberately does not include

  • An X bookmarks import. Not because it is impossible, but because it needs a decision about money first (decision 4). If it goes ahead it is a phase of its own — a third sign-in provider, a paged sync, a spend cap, a per-user bookmark cap, and the refresh-or-expire job from §7.
  • An X DM bot. $0.025 per save, against WhatsApp's $0.
  • Anything requiring X Enterprise.
  • Reading anyone's WhatsApp history. It does not exist.
In plain English. Day one: make X cards stop looking broken. Week one: give Allkept a WhatsApp number people can forward links to — free, and no waiting on Meta this time. Half a day somewhere in there: download your own X archive and look inside, because what is in it decides whether the expensive option is even needed. Then, if you want it, make X posts play inside the app — which also fixes the legal question about how you are allowed to show a tweet.

10. Decisions for Pranav

  1. Is X a platform Allkept promises, or one it merely handles? Today the welcome screen says "Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and the web" — X links work, but nothing tells anyone so. Naming X costs a line of copy and a Phase 1 that makes the card worth showing.
  2. WhatsApp number door: yes or no? It is free, it needs no App Review, and it is the one door here that no competitor has bothered with. It costs a phone number, about a week, and a phone-number line in the privacy policy you removed one eleven days ago. My recommendation: yes, after Phase 1.
  3. Same Meta app as Instagram, or a second one? Reusing it is less setup; a second one means a WhatsApp problem can never put the Instagram App Review at risk while that review is still in flight (forlater.md item 8). I would use a second app until Instagram is approved.
  4. X bookmarks import: build it, or wait? $0.005 per bookmark, roughly $2.50 for a typical person, charged on a free action, unrecoverable in India. It also drags in a third sign-in provider and a 24-hour deletion job. My recommendation: do Phase 3 first — if likes turn out to be in the free archive, the API may be unnecessary.
  5. Phase 4 (play X posts) before or after the store submission? It is the fix for the Display Requirements finding as well as for the missing picture, which argues for before. It is also two to three days of native-adjacent work on a WebView, which argues for after.
  6. WhatsApp chat-export import: do we want it at all? It works, it is free, and it hands Allkept a file full of other people's private messages. That is a principles question before it is an engineering one.
  7. The 24-hour deletion job. X needs it for stored Posts; YouTube needs something like it within 30 days (forlater.md item 17). Build one generic refresh-or-expire mechanism for every platform, or keep deferring both?
